01 / Augmented interactions

Less repetition.
More connection.

"Can you do it? By when? At what price?"
Her agent can begin the conversation. She decides where it goes.

The aim is to answer recurring questions, understand a household's needs, and negotiate within boundaries she sets. A considered proposal comes back to her for approval—without interrupting every hour of work or rest.

Why a conversation matters

A listing with a fixed price cannot hold what a day of work actually depends on: her hours, her travel, her minimum, the household's budget. A conversation between two agents can.

Her rules evolve. So do her availability and the scope of a job. The agent carries them into every negotiation, and she steps in whenever she wants.

02 / Worker-owned social capital

She earned the trust.
It should stay hers.

A platform account should not be the only home for her working life.

Her completed jobs, the households' countersignatures, and the goodwill she builds are part of the value she creates. Every agreement, visit and payment is signed on her phone and chained together, so it cannot be edited behind her back or deleted when she leaves.

What should travel with her?

A useful record holds completed work, countersigned receipts, and the terms both sides agreed to, with their dates. It never holds a star rating.

She chooses what to share with a new household, a union, a helpline or a lender, keeps a copy, and can take it with her when she moves.

03 / Contract writing

Common ground.
Before the work begins.

A price means little if two people imagine different outcomes.

An agent-assisted agreement makes the hours, the weekly off, the leave and the payment schedule explicit. Both phones sign the same terms, and only then does her number move. The household pays her directly by UPI; GigSync never holds a rupee.

From expectations to an agreement

Start with the work and what a finished visit means. Add the days, the hours and the rate. For a longer job, pay by stage, with a countersigned receipt at each one.

If expectations diverge, use a defined path: raise it, compare the work with the agreed terms, and bring the union's steward in if needed.

06 / Built around real life

Familiar ways in.
Personal boundaries intact.

Conversations with workers are shaping the product. These are the principles guiding what we build next.

01Next interfaceMeet people where they already are.WhatsApp

A household signs the terms and pays from a WhatsApp or SMS link without installing anything. The link carries a notice and nothing else; the terms, the contact cards and the record never pass through Meta's servers.

02Access by designIntelligence within reach.Local models

One small open model, under 3B parameters, runs on her own phone and only helps with wording and speech. Matching, negotiation and the ledger are plain rules. The app is complete with no model at all, on a 4 GB phone, offline.

03Privacy by defaultShare what is relevant. Keep the rest hers.Her permission

Private context stays on her device. Messages between agents travel sealed through a mailbox the server cannot read.

"Are you free on Tuesday?"

Her agent shares an approved time window—not her full calendar, her home address, or her number.

Each interaction has a defined scope. Her number and address move only after both phones sign, and only until the job ends.

08 / A future we want to grow

New city.
A familiar circle.

Sometimes a new beginning starts with one person you know.

Workers have told us about moving to a city because a brother, a friend, or someone from their village was already there. A familiar person can make an unfamiliar world feel safer. Most of the women we spoke to found their first work through someone, not through an app.

Beyond work, we hope to grow circles of belonging: workers from the same region and trade, run by their union, with a mentor who opts in; a vouched bed for the first nights; the registrations and documents she is owed. GigSync introduces; the circle does the rest.
