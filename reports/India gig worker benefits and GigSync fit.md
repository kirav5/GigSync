# India's gig workers get paper rights; GigSync must pick its fight

_Research report, 27 October 2026. Synthesised from six research notes (platforms, government and law, worker-support organisations, the migrant journey, worker-held data and agents, and GigSync's own repository). Every claim carries its source; items the researchers could not verify are marked as such. Repository citations use file paths and line numbers as of commit `90aa4a0`._

As of 27 October 2026 an Indian gig worker's real, claimable benefits are thin and almost entirely conditional on staying active on one platform: five companies (Zomato/Blinkit, Swiggy, Urban Company, bigbasket, Zepto) pay for accident and health cover with loss-of-pay, ride-hailing and logistics platforms have shifted cost back onto drivers through subscriptions, and the statutory layer that was supposed to replace this goodwill is mostly unfunded. The Code on Social Security took effect on 21 November 2025 and its Central Rules on 8 May 2026, but the aggregator contribution rate, its start date and every Section 114 benefit scheme remain un-notified, so no central money flows; the only statutory levy actually being collected is Karnataka's 1% fee, effective 13 February 2026, and it is sitting in a High Court registry pending litigation ([KSMG](https://ksmg.co/explainers/labour-codes/gig-worker-social-security-new-labour-codes); [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/high-court/karnataka-high-court/karnataka-high-court-refuses-to-stay-state-gig-workers-act-asks-platforms-to-deposit-welfare-fund-with-court-539865)). Roughly nine in ten of the Economic Survey's **12 million** gig workers are not even in e-Shram, the registry that gates every central and most state benefits (~11.5 lakh platform workers registered as of August 2026) ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/union-budget/story/india-has-12-million-gig-workers-2-of-total-workforce-economic-survey-513429-2026-01-29); [Organiser](https://organiser.org/2026/08/29/377578/bharat/five-years-of-e-shram-31-89-crore-unorganised-workers-join-indias-digital-social-security-net/)). Nobody, platform, state or startup, serves the migrant's first two weeks in a new city: housing runs through village landlords without contracts, the one paying "home away from home" product is a ₹30 crore-ARR hostel startup, and worker-support capital has consolidated into employer-paid jobs marketplaces and ride-hailing co-ops. Against this, GigSync is a **G0 design** with **5,349 lines** of inherited code, zero users, a stub OTP that accepts any code, and documents that describe a job-agreement-and-ledger tool, not the end-to-end life companion the founders now pitch; migration, housing, peer networks, documents, insurance and credit appear nowhere in the README, HLD, LLD or twelve ADRs (`README.md:9`; `gigsync_project_summary.md §8`). Yet GigSync's narrow design occupies real white space that no competitor, government or global precedent fills: a worker-held, counter-signed, machine-readable record of off-platform work; a contact-withholding agreement flow for domestic and home-service jobs that Karnataka's draft Domestic Workers Bill (15 October 2025) may soon make legally mandatory; and an on-device architecture with no platform in the middle ([IndiaLaw](https://www.indialaw.in/blog/labour/karnataka-domestic-workers-bill-2025/)). The recommendation that follows is to build that record first, with one named union in one city, to partner rather than build for housing, registration and peer networks, and to drop the job board, the ONDC relay and the Bluesky onboarding step.

## Statutory money exists on paper; only Karnataka's 1% fee actually flows

The four Labour Codes, including the Code on Social Security 2020, came into force on **21 November 2025**, giving gig and platform workers their first legal definition and a scheme-based social-security architecture separate from EPF, ESI and gratuity, which still cover only "employees" ([DD News](https://ddnews.gov.in/en/what-indias-new-labour-codes-mean-for-gig-and-platform-workers/); [Chambers](https://chambers.com/articles/epf-compliance-in-the-gig-economy-is-indias-new-social-security-framework-enough-2)). The Social Security (Central) Rules 2026 followed on **8 May 2026**, requiring aggregators to register on a central portal and upload their existing workforce within 45 days, a deadline the Ministry refused to extend past 21 June 2026 ([MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/05/223-social-security-central-rules-2026-gig-workers-90-days-work/); [Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/12-lakh-gig-workers-20-aggregators-registered-on-e-shram-portal-so-far-labour-ministry/)). The money, however, has not started. Section 114 sets the aggregator contribution at **1–2% of annual turnover, capped at 5% of payouts to workers**, but the rate and the commencement date both require separate notifications that, as of the most rigorous secondary source found, have not been issued, and no scheme for life, disability, accident, health, maternity or old-age cover has been notified under Section 114(1) ([KSMG](https://ksmg.co/explainers/labour-codes/gig-worker-social-security-new-labour-codes)). The eligibility test built into the Rules, **90 days with one aggregator or 120 days across several in the previous financial year**, has already been criticised as a threshold that "risks excluding the majority of the workforce the rules claim to protect" ([MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/05/223-social-security-central-rules-2026-gig-workers-90-days-work/)). The researchers could not locate a Gazette notification for either the rate or the start date through October 2026; this should be checked on egazette before anyone tells a worker they are "covered".

What a worker actually gets today from the centre is registration. e-Shram holds **31.89 crore** unorganised workers as of 18 August 2026, but only about **11.5 lakh** of them are platform or gig workers, 0.36% of the registry and well under a tenth of the 12 million gig workforce ([Organiser](https://organiser.org/2026/08/29/377578/bharat/five-years-of-e-shram-31-89-crore-unorganised-workers-join-indias-digital-social-security-net/); [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/union-budget/story/india-has-12-million-gig-workers-2-of-total-workforce-economic-survey-513429-2026-01-29)). The platform-worker count jumped from about 5.12 lakh on 1 December 2025 to 10.5 lakh by 23 January 2026, coinciding with the Codes' commencement, then plateaued near 12 lakh through June and August 2026 even as a Ministry official complained that aggregators "have not yet registered their entire workforce" ([Sightsinplus](https://sightsinplus.com/news/labour-law/gig-workers-to-get-insurance-and-pension-under-new-2026-rules); [MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/01/223-maharashtra-e-shram-platform-workers-small-states-lag/); [Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/12-lakh-gig-workers-20-aggregators-registered-on-e-shram-portal-so-far-labour-ministry/)). Registration does give something concrete: a 12-digit UAN, PMSBY accident cover of **₹2 lakh** for a ₹20 annual premium, and a "One-Stop Solution" (21 October 2024) that integrates 15 schemes including PM-SYM's ₹3,000 monthly pension after 60 and One Nation One Ration Card ([Organiser](https://organiser.org/2026/08/29/377578/bharat/five-years-of-e-shram-31-89-crore-unorganised-workers-join-indias-digital-social-security-net/)). The Budget 2025-26 promise of gig-worker ID cards and PM-JAY health cover worth ₹5 lakh per family was described in May 2026 as "not yet launched for platform workers", and no source confirms cards or PM-JAY enrolments issued to gig workers specifically as of October 2026 ([MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/05/223-social-security-central-rules-2026-gig-workers-90-days-work/); [India TV](https://www.indiatvnews.com/business/budget/budget-2025-identity-cards-registration-on-e-shram-portal-for-1-crore-gig-workers-says-sitharaman-2025-02-01-974092)).

The states have moved faster than the centre but only one has a working stack. Six states have statutes or passed bills: Rajasthan (24 July 2023), Karnataka (ordinance May 2025, Act notified 12 September 2025), Bihar (12 August 2025), Jharkhand (Act published 6 January 2026), Telangana (passed 30 March 2026, Act 21 of 2026) and Maharashtra (bill introduced 3 July 2026) ([PRS Rajasthan](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_states/rajasthan/2023/Act29of2023Rajasthan.pdf); [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/employee-rights-labour-relations/1713454/karnataka-platform-based-gig-workers-social-security-and-welfare-act-2025); [PRS Bihar](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_states/bihar/2025/Act8of2025BR.pdf); [TeamLease RegTech](https://www.teamleaseregtech.com/updates/article/54306/jharkhand-platform-based-gig-workers-registration-and-welfare-act-2025/); [PRS Telangana](https://prsindia.org/files/bills_acts/acts_states/telangana/2026/Act21of2026TG.pdf); [TeamLease RegTech](https://www.teamleaseregtech.com/updates/article/57603/maharashtra-gig-and-platform-workers-rights-and-protection-bill-2026/)). Only **Karnataka** has rules (19 November 2025 and 31 March 2026), a constituted Board (January 2026), a notified fee (1% of each payout with per-transaction caps of ₹0.50 to ₹1.50, effective 13 February 2026) and a live grievance portal (IPGRS, May 2026) that routes a complaint to the platform's Internal Dispute Resolution Committee, demands an action-taken report in 14 working days and a reasoned order in 45 ([SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/02/18/karnataka-government-notifies-gig-workers-welfare-fee-mandatory/); [South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/karnataka/karnataka-platform-based-gig-workers-act-2025-rules-notified-board-to-meet-at-least-once-every-three-months/); [Chambers](https://chambers.com/articles/digitizing-justice-grievance-redressal-for-platform-based-gig-workers-in-karnataka)). Karnataka's Act also gives workers two rights no other jurisdiction operationalises: Section 13's right to ask, in a language they know, how automated systems set fares, ratings and task allocation, and Section 14(2)'s bar on deactivation without written reasons and 14 days' notice ([MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2025/08/223-karnataka-gig-worker-law-algorithmic-transparency-gaps/)). Even this is contested: IAMAI, Eternal, Zepto, Swiggy, Urban Company and Valmo petitioned the Karnataka High Court on 27 June 2026, which on 3 July 2026 refused a stay but ordered the second-quarter fee deposited with the court registry rather than the Board ([Medianama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/06/223-swiggy-zepto-challenge-karnataka-gig-workers-law/); [LiveLaw](https://www.livelaw.in/high-court/karnataka-high-court/karnataka-high-court-refuses-to-stay-state-gig-workers-act-asks-platforms-to-deposit-welfare-fund-with-court-539865)). Rajasthan's first-mover law stalled when the government changed and rules were never framed; Tamil Nadu's board, formed without a statute in December 2023, had enrolled 6,700 workers, 0.7% of the workforce, by May 2025 because aggregators would not share data; Delhi has a ₹10 crore budget line and an announcement ([Labour Review](https://labourreview.org/beyond-welfare/); [DT Next](https://www.dtnext.in/news/tamilnadu/tn-gig-workers-welfare-hits-aggregator-roadblock-sees-below-1-enrolment-834335); [Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/delhi/govt-to-set-up-welfare-board-for-gig-platform-workers-10-cr-allocated)).

Two structural features of this landscape matter for any worker-side tool. First, every state scheme keys registration to the platform submitting a worker database, and every state reports that data-sharing as its bottleneck; a worker who can prove platform engagement without waiting for the aggregator has leverage nowhere else available. Second, entitlements do not travel: a worker who moves from Bengaluru to Hyderabad leaves behind a state UIN, a fee-funded board and a grievance route, and the East Asia Forum's assessment is that board entitlements "expire at each administrative boundary" ([East Asia Forum](https://eastasiaforum.org/2026/04/09/indias-gig-economy-is-growing-faster-than-its-protections/)). India's DPDP Act 2023 offers no help here: it grants access, correction and erasure rights but **no data-portability right**, so an Indian worker cannot compel a platform to hand over machine-readable records the way the Amsterdam Court of Appeal compelled Uber and Ola in April 2023 ([Jindal Policy Research Lab](https://jgu.edu.in/jsgp/jindal-policy-research-lab/digital-personal-data-protection-act-2023/); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/04/05/uber-ola-gdpr-worker-data-access-rights-appeal/)).

## Five platforms insure active workers; none houses, relocates or represents them

Fairwork India's latest edition (October 2024; 11 platforms, 440 interviews, five cities) is the only independent anchor, and no 2025 edition could be retrieved despite one December 2025 press item implying it exists. bigbasket, Swiggy, Urban Company and Zomato scored **6/10**; BluSmart 5; Zepto 4; Amazon Flex 2; Flipkart 1; **Ola, Porter and Uber 0** ([Fairwork India 2024 PDF](https://citapp.iiitb.ac.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Fairwork-India-2024-Report.pdf)). Only bigbasket and Urban Company had a policy guaranteeing the hourly local minimum wage after work-related costs, no platform committed to a living wage, and no platform earned a single Representation point because none would recognise a collective body of workers ([Fairwork India 2024 PDF](https://citapp.iiitb.ac.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Fairwork-India-2024-Report.pdf)).

The benefit stack divides sharply by sector. Food and grocery delivery and home services carry a real company-paid insurance layer. Zomato and Blinkit offer accident cover up to ₹10 lakh, medical cover of ₹1 lakh with OPD, loss-of-pay up to ₹50,000 and maternity up to ₹40,000, and spent over **₹100 crore** on premiums in 2025; they also run two paid period-rest days a month for women, a gig-specific NPS variant with 54,000 enrolments, and over 5,000 open-to-all rest points as of May 2026 ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/latest/corporate/story/rs100-crore-on-insurance-flexible-hours-deepinder-goyal-makes-case-for-zomatos-gig-economy-model-509255-2026-01-02); [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/amp/story/news/amazon-swiggy-eternal-heatwave-relief-delivery-partners)). Swiggy renewed cover for over **25 lakh** partners in FY26 with ₹10 lakh accidental death, ₹2 lakh hospitalisation and maternity cover that extends to spouses, facilitated ₹102 crore in loans in the twelve months to December 2023, and signed an upGrad skilling MoU in November 2025 ([FM Live](https://www.fmlive.in/swiggy-renews-comprehensive-insurance-programme-for-its-delivery-partners-with-indusind-general-insurance-over-25-lakh-delivery-partners-covered-under-insurance-benefits-in-fy26/); [Swiggy press release](https://www.swiggy.com/corporate/press-release/swiggy-facilitated-the-disbursement-of-rs-102-crores-in-loans-to-its-delivery-partners-in-the-last-12-months/); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/companies/news/swiggy-signs-mou-with-upgrad-for-skill-development-of-delivery-partners-125111701109_1.html)). Urban Company gives all active partners group life up to ₹10 lakh and disability up to ₹6 lakh, reports average net in-hand earnings of **₹28,322 a month** for the nine months to December 2025, runs 300+ trainers under an NSDC partnership, and remains the only platform with a partner stock plan (₹150 crore PSOP announced March 2022, about 500 partners by FY23, no later grant figures found) ([UC Investor Relations](https://investorrelations.urbancompany.com/announcements-and-highlights/service-partners-earnings-index-9m-fy26); [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2022/03/urban-company-announces-rs-150-cr-partner-stock-ownership-plan-psop)). Amazon's Project Ashray rest centres target 250 by end-2026 and its 90,000+ last-mile associates got accident cover raised to ₹10 lakh in May 2026 ([About Amazon India](https://www.aboutamazon.in/news/operations/amazon-flexible-delivery-jobs); [StartupTalky](https://startuptalky.com/news/amazon-india-boosts-health-benefits/)).

Ride-hailing and logistics have gone the other way. Ola drivers since June 2025 keep 100% of fares but pay **₹67 a day** (₹2,010 a month) for the privilege; Rapido has charged daily SaaS fees since February 2023; Uber's only documented driver insurance is a 2017 ICICI Lombard scheme whose current status could not be confirmed and whose official page returns 404; Porter's September 2025 driver terms make the driver solely responsible for vehicle insurance and state the company is "under no circumstances liable" ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/ola-drivers-need-to-pay-inr-2010-a-month-to-avail-zero-commission-model/); [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/companies/start-ups/after-cabs-rapido-extends-saas-based-zero-commission-model-to-auto-drivers-124021300846_1.html); [Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/2017/08/uber-partners-icici-lombard/); [Porter T&C PDF](https://nest-platform-assets.porter.in/Smart_Shift_Logistics_Driver_Partner_T_and_C_Updated_on_20_09_2025_1_be5cd3f264/Smart_Shift_Logistics_Driver_Partner_T_and_C_Updated_on_20_09_2025_1_be5cd3f264.pdf)). The Motor Vehicle Aggregator Guidelines 2025 require ₹5 lakh health and ₹10 lakh term cover per driver, but unions struck on 7 February 2026 partly because base fares under those guidelines were still not notified ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/india/story/all-india-breakdown-why-are-ola-uber-rapido-drivers-going-on-strike-list-of-demands-the-nitin-gadkari-connection-and-more-514925-2026-02-06)).

Three caveats run through every platform benefit. Cover is tied to being active, so it lapses exactly when a worker is injured, relocating or between platforms; unions in Telangana say platform insurance has no cashless option and workers pay out of pocket, one study found only one in five gig workers has life or health cover, and a documented denial (Reliance "silver plan") shows claims fail in practice ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/features/insurance-promises-vs-ground-reality-gig-workers-push-back-on-zomato-swiggy-claims); [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/corporate/the-unprotected-millions-bridging-the-health-insurance-gap-for-indias-gig-workforce); [Rest of World](https://restofworld.org/2024/swiggy-health-insurance-quotas/)). Marketing figures exceed disclosed policy terms: Blinkit's "up to ₹10 lakh medical insurance", Zepto's "₹10 lakhs" and Rapido's "₹5 lakh" are careers-page copy whose insurers and terms were not retrievable, while audited disclosures put medical cover at ₹1–2 lakh and the headline figure is almost always accidental death ([blinkit.com](https://blinkit.com/pages/delivery); [rapido.bike](https://www.rapido.bike/become-a-captain)). And what workers strike about is procedural, not actuarial: the New Year's Eve 2025 log-off (organisers claimed over 200,000 participants) and the 7 February 2026 "All India Breakdown" demanded pay floors, transparent payout algorithms, an end to arbitrary ID blocking and recognition, none of which an insurance product fixes ([CNN](https://edition.cnn.com/2026/01/01/india/india-gig-workers-delivery-strike-intl-hnk); [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/india/story/all-india-breakdown-why-are-ola-uber-rapido-drivers-going-on-strike-list-of-demands-the-nitin-gadkari-connection-and-more-514925-2026-02-06)). Blinkit's response to a Varanasi heat protest in April–May 2025 was to block about 150 partners' IDs and reinstate most only after they signed an undertaking permitting company action for future protests ([BHRRC](https://www.business-humanrights.org/en/latest-news/india-blinkit-accused-of-retaliating-against-gig-workers-by-blocking-ids-after-protest-over-wages-and-work-hours)).

The domestic and home-service segment GigSync targets is the newest and harshest. Instant-help bookings rose roughly sixfold from about 8 lakh a month in October 2025 to over 50 lakh by July 2026, with Pronto raising $45 million and Snabbit $56 million while all three leaders stay unprofitable; Urban Company's InstaHelp promised ₹20,000 a month for 132 logged hours, then moved to rating-based tiers, and workers say the "assured salary" no longer exists ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/features/the-ground-reality-for-the-gig-workers-powering-indias-instant-help-boom/)). Thirty-five women on Mumbai's instant-help apps described unpaid leave, fines of ₹1,000–2,000 for weekend absence, caste-based treatment, an account blocked for nursing a sick child and an inability to block specific customers; women on Snabbit and Urban Company who reported molestation found their "only immediate recourse is to inform the platform, whose default response is to block the users" ([Scroll](https://scroll.in/article/1091102/why-domestic-workers-say-gig-work-platforms-have-let-them-down); [ThePrint](https://theprint.in/ground-reports/snabbit-urban-company-women-gig-workers-molested-delhi-bengaluru/2877171)). No platform of any kind offers housing, relocation or arrival support; the research found exactly one third-party operator, Nia.one, running about 100 four-to-six-bed "studios" in Delhi, Pune, Bengaluru and Chennai at **₹1,500 rent plus ₹1,500 meals** a month, with 10,000 members, ₹30 crore ARR in FY26 and a $2.5 million seed round ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/startups/how-nia-is-fixing-indias-gig-economics-by-giving-workers-a-home-away-from-home/)).

## Worker-support capital sells to employers; co-ops and unions stay vertical and tool-poor

The best-funded companies that nominally serve blue-collar workers monetise the employer. Apna became a unicorn at $1.1 billion in 2021 and processed 14 lakh job postings and 73 lakh AI-interview sessions in 2025, but over half its listed jobs are now white-collar and FY25 total revenue dipped even as recruitment revenue grew 61% to ₹57.1 crore ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/economy/blue-collar-workers-app-apna-becomes-a-unicorn-in-21-months-valued-at-1-1-billion/734273/); [NewsBytes](https://www.newsbytesapp.com/news/business/apna-uses-ai-to-match-blue-collar-workers-aims-rs150cr-revenue/tldr); [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/apna-narrows-fy25-loss-to-inr-50-cr-even-as-revenue-dips)). WorkIndia raised ₹97 crore in December 2025 on FY25 revenue of ₹78.7 crore; Vahan places about 40,000 workers a month into delivery via a WhatsApp bot and took Temasek-linked money in July 2025 ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/news/workindia-raises-rs-97-cr-in-series-b-led-by-aavishkaar-capital-10922965); [Venture Intelligence](https://news.ventureintelligence.com/ma/blue-collar-workers-hiring-platform-vahan.ai-raises-funding-from-temasek’s-lemmatree;-acquires-upski)). The task-work layer has consolidated outright: Awign became a Mynavi subsidiary on 25 April 2024, Taskmo went to Quess in April 2024 and GigIndia to PhonePe in March 2022 ([Mynavi](https://www.mynavi.jp/eng/news/2024/04/post_43355.html); [CB Insights](https://www.cbinsights.com/compare/gigindia-vs-taskmo)). Gig fintech is either employer-gated (Refyne's earned-wage access reaches workers only through 500+ employer contracts; Onsurity and Plum sell group health to employers) or dormant (KarmaLife, which claimed 2.7 lakh workers served in 2023, has no 2025–26 coverage; Bimaplan and Siply likewise) ([Techleap](https://finder.techleap.nl/news/feed/refyne-secures-inr-35-cr-debt-funding); [YourStory](https://yourstory.com/2023/06/funding-karmalife-44-crore-pre-series-a-round)). Lenders trust two income sources, the platform via API link and the bank via Account Aggregator, and the RBI Innovation Hub's December 2024 pilot with Vivifi Finance pulls gig-platform activity under consent, a path that by construction excludes off-platform work; no lender was found that accepts a worker-held or platform-independent record ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/rbi-innovation-hub-pilots-unsecured-loan-offerings-for-gig-workers); [CGAP PDF](https://www.cgap.org/sites/default/files/publications/Putting%20Gig%20Data%20To%20Work.pdf)).

The only direct-to-worker models with scale are worker-first ride-hailing apps, and every one is a drivers-only vertical. Namma Yatri had **4 lakh drivers** across eight cities by July 2024 on a ₹25-a-day subscription, though its non-Bengaluru cities have "yet to find comparable success"; West Bengal's state-owned Yatri Sathi reports 1.15 lakh drivers and ₹305 crore in cumulative driver earnings as of June 2025; and the Ministry of Cooperation's Bharat Taxi, launched 5 February 2026, claimed **4.31 lakh** shareholder-driver members by 23 March 2026 with promises of health insurance, accident cover, retirement savings and cheap loans bundled into membership ([Antler](https://www.antler.co/press-releases/namma-yatri-raises-11-million-to-revolutionize-mobility-and-build-seamless-public-transportation); [Rest of World](https://restofworld.org/2025/uber-ola-copy-india-zero-commission-ride-hailing-app/); [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/karnataka/namma-yatri-to-offer-auto-rides-in-6-more-cities-in-karnataka-by-year-end-2981497); [Millennium Post](https://www.millenniumpost.in/bengal/cab-drivers-earnings-via-yatri-sathi-cross-rs-300-cr-since-inception-in-2023-615022); [Indian Cooperative](https://www.indiancooperative.com/co-op-news-snippets/bharat-taxi-onboards-3-76-lakh-drivers/amp/); [Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/india/story/sarathi-hi-malik-how-bharat-taxi-debut-aims-to-disrupt-ride-hailing-economics-514865-2026-02-05)). Bharat Taxi's roughly 10,000 rides a day on 4 lakh drivers implies very low utilisation and all its figures are government claims, but the direction is clear: for drivers, the state and the co-operative movement are already bundling the benefits a worker-first app might have offered ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/india/governance/amit-shah-launches-bharat-taxi-the-govt-backed-alternative-to-ola-uber-drivers-will-be-owners/2847000)). Namma Yatri's Covrzy tie-up (June 2025) embeds driver-paid health plans in-app with a target of 50,000 drivers by 2026 ([BW Disrupt](https://www.bwdisrupt.com/article/covrzy-namma-yatri-tie-up-to-offer-health-insurance-access-to-50000-gig-workers-by-2026-559185)). No cross-sector equivalent exists for delivery, domestic work or construction.

Unions are the most credible worker-side actors and the least tooled. IFAT and TGPWU co-led the December 2025 to February 2026 strike wave; TGPWU reports over 1,000 active and over 10,000 registered members, and its 2024 impact report found that **62% of surveyed workers got support from the union itself**, scholarships, ration kits, accident-insurance help, e-Shram and ITR filing, rather than from platforms ([TGPWU](https://tgpwu.org/?p=2); [BHRRC](https://www.business-humanrights.org/en/latest-news/india-unions-report-highlights-gig-and-platform-workers-struggles-with-wage-theft-unfair-deactivations-lack-of-social-security-unsafe-working-conditions/)). TGPWU's 2023 impact report explicitly demanded that workers have "full access to all data collected about them, along with the right to download or transfer that data", the clearest documented demand signal for a worker-held record, though framed as access to the platform's data rather than a worker-generated ledger ([TGPWU Impact Report 2023 PDF](https://tgpwu.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/TGPWU_ImpactEvaluationReport_2023_Digital-1.pdf)). Unions publish PDFs and press releases and coordinate on WhatsApp; no membership, dues, grievance-logging or evidence tooling was found. Janpahal's February 2026 occupational-safety survey of over 1,000 workers in ten metros found more than half working over 49 hours a week, about a quarter over 70, and roughly half without accident or health insurance ([The Wire](https://m.thewire.in/article/economy/more-than-20-gig-workers-work-70-hours-per-week-report)). The Economic Survey 2025-26 adds that **40% of gig workers earn under ₹15,000 a month** and recommends per-hour pay floors including wait time, algorithm transparency and portable social security ([Upstox](https://upstox.com/news/business-news/latest-updates/40-of-gig-workers-earn-under-15-000-a-month-economic-survey-pitches-pay-floors-algorithm-checks/article-188520)).

Global precedent is sobering for anyone building worker-held data tools. Driver's Seat Cooperative's cross-platform earnings app has been absorbed into Princeton's Workers' Algorithm Observatory and its website returned 404 on 27 October 2026; MIT's Mozilla-funded Gigbox is marked "put to rest"; Rodeo, the UK courier-earnings app, was blocked by Deliveroo via a "security update" in March 2023 and has no 2025–26 news; the one durable organisation, Worker Info Exchange, does not give workers a ledger but files GDPR requests and litigates, winning the April 2023 Amsterdam ruling that a PDF export does not satisfy "structured, common, machine-readable" ([App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/drivers-seat/id6478721051); [Civic Tech Field Guide](https://civictech.guide/projects/gigbox); [UKTN](https://www.uktech.news/mobility/deliveroo-blocks-rodeo-20230321); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/04/05/uber-ola-gdpr-worker-data-access-rights-appeal/)). The pattern is that technology was never the bottleneck; projects died on platform blocking, community-building and grant dependence. Equally, no deployed product anywhere lets a worker's own agent negotiate with a counterparty's agent: 2026's "agentic commerce" is checkout protocol work, and the only Indian voice agents aimed at blue-collar workers (Vahan, Hunar) are employer-side recruiters ([SoFi](https://www.sofi.com/learn/content/how-agentic-commerce-is-transforming-online-shopping/); [NITI Frontier Tech](https://frontiertech.niti.gov.in/story/voice-ai-at-scale-powering-half-a-million-jobs-a-year-in-india/)). On-device Indic speech is now feasible at 200–300 MB per language (Sarvam Edge, 14 February 2026; AI4Bharat IndicConformer INT8 ports), though no benchmark on sub-₹10,000 Android phones was found ([Analytics Vidhya](https://www.analyticsvidhya.com/blog/2026/03/sarvam-edge/); [Sherpa-ONNX port](https://good23hfaccelerating.pages.dev/meetsync/indic-conformer-onnx-sherpa)).

## The migrant's first two weeks cost ₹2,500–3,500 and no one serves them

Half the delivery workforce is migrant. IDinsight's 2024 phone survey of 2,547 two-wheeler delivery drivers found **50–51% migrants, split 24% intra-state and 26% inter-state**, with over half of drivers in large metros being migrants; the average driver is 28, nets **₹18,761 a month after 32% expenses for a 62-hour week**, and has been on the platform about 13 months ([IDinsight](https://www.idinsight.org/article/the-changing-landscape-of-work-insights-into-indias-delivery-platform-gig-workforce/)). The "migrant" label hides a split that matters for product design: an ISEC policy brief (December 2025, sample size unverified because the PDF could not be parsed) puts Bengaluru's gig-platform migrants at 66% from north Karnataka and only about 14% from other states, so the typical Bengaluru newcomer is a Kannada speaker with the same ration card and bank state, whereas Delhi-NCR draws over 70% of its migrants from UP, Bihar and Jharkhand and Kerala's delivery migrants come from Assam, Bengal, Odisha and Jharkhand ([ISEC PB-73](https://www.isec.ac.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/PB-73-Governance-of-Gig-Platform-Economy.pdf); [The Quint](https://www.thequint.com/jobs/delhi-assembly-elections-2025-arvind-kejriwal-aap-bjp-jobs-unemployment-data-latest-news); [The South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/keralam/delivery-gigs-migrants-entry-into-keralams-informal-labour-market-with-little-safety-net/)). Short tenure means the arrival problem recurs constantly; the population churns.

Housing runs entirely through origin-based networks and village landlords. The academic literature on Gurgaon's informal rental market finds that "social networks that carry over from places of origin" drive housing choice, that village landlords control the stock, and that roughly a quarter of India's housing is informal rental without contracts ([NIUA – Naik 2015](https://smartnet.niua.org/sites/default/files/resources/10.1177_0975425315591425.pdf); [CPR India](https://cprindia.org/journalarticles/informal-rental-housing-typologies-and-experiences-of-low-income-migrant-renters-in-gurgaon-india/)). Reported conditions range from 12–20 workers in a 10x10 ft room in Bengaluru labour colonies (anecdotal) to a Zomato rider's ₹500-a-month windowless Mumbai slum room (a single viral video), while formal PG beds in Bengaluru run **₹8,000–15,000**, priced for white-collar tenants and unaffordable on ₹18,761 net ([Citizen Matters](https://citizenmatters.in/section/migrant-workers/); [NewsBytes](https://www.newsbytesapp.com/news/india/zomato-delivery-agents-rs500-room-in-mumbai-slum-goes-viral-woman-pays-his-rent-for-3-months/story); [Ghar.tv](https://www.ghar.tv/blog/best-pg-in-bengaluru-2025-complete-guide-to-finding-perfect-paying-guest-accommodation/artid3422)). The state's answer, Affordable Rental Housing Complexes, had converted 5,648 of a targeted 83,534 flats as of 2023, none in Delhi or Maharashtra, and its official portal's TLS certificate had expired when fetched on 27 October 2026 ([IMPRI](https://www.impriindia.com/insights/affordable-rental-housing-scheme-3/); [MoHUA ARHC](https://arhc.mohua.gov.in/)). Platform "rest points" (Zomato 5,000+, Swiggy Instamart 900+) are daytime shelters, not lodging ([Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/corporate/food-delivery-e-commerce-platforms-unite-to-protect-their-delivery-partners-from-heatwave)). Nia.one's ₹1,500-a-month bed is the only product found that prices for this segment.

The cash gap before the first payout is the binding constraint. Platform documents are standard (Aadhaar or PAN, DL, RC, bank account, smartphone), but a July 2024 viral post priced Swiggy's kit at ₹1,199 with damaged-bag replacement deducted from earnings, third-party guides cite a ₹600 deposit and an onboarding fee around ₹1,500 (both low reliability), and a Yulu e-bike cost ₹1,400 a week in 2022 and roughly ₹9,000 a month by a 2025 rider's account, which is a third to a half of net earnings ([Business Standard](https://business-standard.com/social-viral/does-swiggy-charge-delivery-agents-for-branded-kits-viral-post-says-yes-124072400632_1.html); [Citizen Matters](https://citizenmatters.in/?p=17338); [Mint via Magzter](https://www.magzter.com/en/stories/newspaper/Mint-Mumbai/YULUS-SMALL-EVS-SLOW-SPEED-QUICK-DELIVERY-569650)). The researchers' inference, not a measured figure, is that a newcomer who rents a vehicle and buys a kit needs **₹2,500–3,500 before earning a rupee**, on top of a room deposit. No official source states time-to-first-payout for any platform. On documents, the law is ahead of practice: RBI's 2016 KYC amendment and a 2019 PMLA rule change let a migrant open an account with a self-declared local address, yet reporting shows banks still refuse on address grounds, and no source quantifies how many days Aadhaar update, SIM, bank or police verification take ([BloombergQuint](https://www.bloombergquint.com/aadhaar/govt-eases-norms-for-change-of-address-on-aadhaar); [Hindustan Times via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/india/hindustan-times-jalandhar/20180128/282325385404785)). Background verification takes 15–21 days on average per a 2022 Sattva study ([Sattva PDF](https://www.sattva.co.in/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/Skilling-and-Employment-Potential-for-Gig-Workers-through-DESH-Stack.pdf)).

The journey is also where exploitation concentrates. Delhi Cyber Police busted a placement racket on 4 August 2026 that charged a victim ₹4,800 in "registration and account-opening charges"; a June 2025 raid on a racket trading under the "Job Hai" app name recovered over 100 Aadhaar cards and fake service agreements ([IANS Live](https://ianslive.in/delhi-cyber-police-bust-fake-placement-racket-seven-arrested-for-cheating-job-seekers--20260804130127); [IANS Live](https://ianslive.in/fake-job-racket-busted-in-delhi-police-recover-100-resumes-aadhaar-cards--20250624184853)). The PAIGAM survey of over 10,000 cab and delivery workers in eight cities found **47% of cab drivers and 41.5% of delivery workers had faced violence at work** ([TGPWU](https://tgpwu.org/2024/03/13/prisoners-on-wheels-report/)). The helpline infrastructure is built for a different worker: India Labourline (1800-833-9020, since 2021) handled 3,600+ cases and recovered about ₹2.1 crore by June 2022 and its Kerala chapter logged ₹2.41 crore in claims over 18 months, but its method is phone mediation with a contractor, its caseload is construction and plywood wage theft, and no evidence checklist for a platform dispute exists anywhere ([Scroll](https://scroll.in/article/1029692/rs-2-crore-in-9-months-a-helpline-is-assisting-workers-in-recovering-wages); [inkl](https://inkl.com/news/migrant-workers-lodge-complaints-of-wage-theft-amounting-to-2-41-crore-in-18-months)). The NGO layer (Aajeevika Bureau's walk-in kendras on the Rajasthan–Gujarat corridor, Jan Sahas's Migrants Resilience Collaborative, Gram Vaani's IVR networks) is anchored to sectors and corridors, not to the platform worker's journey ([Aajeevika](https://aajeevika.org/about-us/); [The CSR Journal](https://thecsrjournal.in/indian-philanthropists-migrants-resilience-collaborative-platform-mrc/)). The peer-network step, how a newcomer finds workers from their region, is the least documented and most load-bearing; no study, platform feature or community app for it was found, and the only confirmed referral programme is Rapido's customer-side 50-coin bonus ([Flyy blog](https://www.theflyy.com/blog/rapido-referral-program)).

## GigSync's documents describe a job ledger, not the life the founders pitch

The honest starting point is the gap between what the founders say in conversation and what the repository says. The stated vision is to onboard gig workers and help them end to end: moving city, finding work, finding a place to stay, meeting existing workers. The README's own words are narrower and precise: "GigSync gives a gig worker their own agent on their own phone. The agent agrees each job with the consumer's agent, holds back their number and address until both sides sign, and keeps a signed record of every job and payment that belongs to them" (`README.md:3-6`). The HLD frames the product as Collab "narrowed to one use case", a worker and a consumer with "no employer, platform or agency in the middle", starting with "service workers in India, in domestic work, beauty and home services" (`HLD.md:19-21, 28-30`). A case-insensitive grep of every doc for the vision's themes returns nothing: `migrat` matches only database migrations; `relocat`, `new city`, `hostel`, `accommodation`, `housing`, `rent`, `peer`, `community`, `mentor`, `buddy`, `insurance`, `loan`, `pension`, `esic`, `e-shram`, `social security`, `minimum wage`, `skill` (in the training sense), `sim card` all return zero hits (`gigsync_project_summary.md §8`). Several vision items are not merely absent but implicitly excluded by stated non-goals: "Holding or moving money" rules out advances or wallets, "Multi-tenant SaaS for platforms" rules out helping workers across commercial platforms, and "Ratings or ranking of workers by GigSync" cuts against portable reputation, even though the inherited protocol still carries a 1–5 star `app.gigsync.review` record the docs never reconcile with that non-goal (`HLD.md:34-43`; `packages/protocol/src/records.ts:49-57`). The only worker-to-worker construct is a union-issued "do not accept this customer" flag that shares "never their story"; there is no messaging, grouping, referral or introduction between workers (`HLD.md:79, 226`). "Onboarding" in the docs means GigSync's own sign-up (language, OTP, PIN, recovery code, role), not onboarding to Swiggy or Urban Company (`LLD.md:403`). Welfare boards appear only as recipients of an export; e-Shram, the single most valuable registration a worker can make, is never mentioned (`HLD.md:225`).

The build is a design, and the design's own status line says so: "G0, design" (`README.md:9`). The repository holds **5,349 lines** of TypeScript across 13 test files, "the code below is still Collab's M0 foundation, and the GigSync features in the HLD/LLD are planned" (`README.md:15-16`). There are no users and the app cannot sign in: it uses "a local id kept in settings", its only call to its own API is a health check, and no envelope has ever been sent from the app (`apps/mobile/src/payments/store.ts:31`; `apps/mobile/src/adapters/api.ts:9-17`). The cloud OTP route accepts a `DEV_OTP` that always verifies, envelope signatures are not checked (`// TODO(M2): verify sig`), storage is Preferences-backed rather than SQLCipher, and the Ed25519 key is "DEV ONLY: lives in Preferences" (`workers/api/src/routes/auth.ts:26, 50`; `workers/api/src/index.ts:98`; `apps/mobile/src/adapters/ports.ts:12-13, 43`). The two headline security properties, the PII sanitizer and the encrypted worker-held store, do not exist: ADR 0007 admits "The agent does not call the sanitizer yet" and no `pii/`, ledger hash-chain, export, template or `packages/crypto` directory is present (`docs/adr/0007-sanitize-then-local-model-then-gemini.md:35-36`; `LLD.md:303-345`). The engagement state machine and policy engine are Collab's, with Collab's states and grant topics; the GigSync job machine (`requested, negotiating, active, disputed, ended`), `WorkerRules`, `job.ts`, `receipt.ts`, `evidence.ts`, `flag.ts`, the union D1 migration and the notifier, job-board and ONDC workers are all "Plan" with no file in the repo (`packages/protocol/src/engagement.ts:9-22`; `LLD.md:44-54, 274-301, 454-466`). The on-device model plugin reports "no model" on every platform (`plugins/gigsync-llm/src/web.ts:4-11`). What a worker can do today is type a customer's name and a job description into a local notebook, generate a UPI QR, and see totals (`apps/mobile/src/screens/Jobs.tsx:57-58`; `screens/Record.tsx:7-25`). The G1 agent-to-agent flow exists only as two commits on an unmerged branch, `origin/feat/agent-flow`, carrying ADRs 0013 and 0014 that were not reviewed beyond their headers (`gigsync_project_summary.md §4.3`). There is no licence (`README.md:182`), no named partner union (the HLD says only "with a partner union", `HLD.md:301`), and the Mozilla fit memo referenced in the project's memory note does not exist in any branch; the memory note's own conclusion was that the project "fails Mozilla's 'working technology deployed with users' gate", that the Fellowship is the better fit for a G0 project, and that the Human-Scale AI Incubator deadline is Friday 20 November 2026 (`gigsync_project_summary.md §7`).

Three design choices also work against the founders' own target users. The pilot is meant to run with a Bengaluru or Hyderabad union, yet the planned templates and speech packs cover only Hindi, Telugu and English; Bengaluru's migrant gig workforce is two-thirds Kannada-speaking intra-state movers, and inter-state migrants speak Odia, Bengali, Bhojpuri or Assamese, none of which are planned (`HLD.md:41`; `LLD.md:525-526`; [ISEC PB-73](https://www.isec.ac.in/wp-content/uploads/2026/08/PB-73-Governance-of-Gig-Platform-Economy.pdf)). Every user must connect a Bluesky account at onboarding, which "needs an email" and a CAPTCHA, a step the ADR itself flags as a drop-off risk for a low-literacy user (`docs/adr/0006-bluesky-connect-at-onboarding.md:38, 79`). And the payment design assumes the worker already has a bank account and UPI ID, which is exactly what a newly arrived migrant often lacks and what banks still obstruct in practice (`docs/adr/0011-upi-qr-payments-no-gateway.md:13`; [Hindustan Times via PressReader](https://www.pressreader.com/india/hindustan-times-jalandhar/20180128/282325385404785)). Voice-first on a ₹10,000 phone requires a 130 MB per-language download on Wi-Fi, and the WebView-on-4-GB performance assumption is explicitly unvalidated (`HLD.md:273`; `docs/adr/0001-capacitor-one-codebase.md:20-21`).

The competitive picture adds pressure the docs do not acknowledge. For drivers, Bharat Taxi and state boards are already bundling insurance, loans and IDs into membership; for delivery workers, five platforms run company-paid cover and the Karnataka Board will give registered workers a dashboard of "payments, welfare fees and eligible schemes", which is a state-run version of GigSync's ledger for on-platform work ([South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/karnataka/karnataka-platform-based-gig-workers-act-2025-rules-notified-board-to-meet-at-least-once-every-three-months/)). For on-platform work, the aggregator already submits engagement data to e-Shram under the May 2026 Rules; a worker-held duplicate adds leverage in a dispute but is not the only record. The global graveyard of worker-data tools shows that building the ledger is the easy part and that community-building, platform resistance and grant dependence are what kill such projects.

## Where GigSync is genuinely alone: off-platform records, domestic agreements, contact-withholding

Having led with what is wrong, the evidence also supports four claims of differentiation, each tied to a specific gap in the notes.

The first is the worker-held, counter-signed, machine-readable record of **off-platform** work. Every record in India today is platform-submitted and state-held (e-Shram, welfare-board UINs) or employer-held (Betterplace, AuthBridge); no startup, union or government body offers a worker-controlled cross-platform record, and the one hackathon prototype (KaushalID, FOSS Hack 2026) is not a product ([DLA Piper](https://knowledge.dlapiper.com/dlapiperknowledge/globalemploymentlatestdevelopments/2026/india-mandates-aggregator-onboarding-to-eshram-portal-to-expand-social-security-coverage); [FOSS United](https://fossunited.org/hack/fosshack26/p/frpfns3bu1)). The regulator's preferred lending path (RBIH/ULI) and e-Shram's aggregator API both exclude the domestic worker paid in cash or UPI by a household, because there is no aggregator to submit anything. For that worker, a record captured at the moment of agreement and countersigned by the customer's phone is the only route to a machine-readable record, and the Amsterdam court has already ruled that a PDF is not one ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/04/05/uber-ola-gdpr-worker-data-access-rights-appeal/)). GigSync's design produces exactly this: an append-only, hash-chained ledger on the phone, receipts countersigned by the consumer's device, unilateral entries kept and marked as such if the customer does not sign within 48 hours, and scoped exports to a union, India Labourline or a welfare board (`HLD.md:177-179, 218-226`; `LLD.md:333`). Two things make the counter-signature matter more than any earnings tracker: a self-attested record is worthless to a lender or mediator, and the research found that the Labourline method is a phone call to the counterparty, which a worker holding the counterparty's own signed terms enters from a far stronger position ([Scroll](https://scroll.in/article/1029692/scroll_in)). Union demand exists in writing: TGPWU asked for download and transfer rights in 2023 ([TGPWU Impact Report 2023 PDF](https://tgpwu.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/TGPWU_ImpactEvaluationReport_2023_Digital-1.pdf)). The caveat is that no Indian court, lender or board has yet accepted a worker-generated record as evidence; acceptance is the hypothesis the pilot must test.

The second is the written-agreement gap in domestic and home-service work, and the regulatory hook now forming around it. Karnataka's draft Domestic Workers (Social Security and Welfare) Bill, released 15 October 2025, would bar engaging any domestic worker without a written agreement on a state-notified model format covering work, hours, wages, leave, welfare fee and termination, would require the employer, worker and any placement agency or digital platform to register the agreement on a government portal within 30 days, and would issue the worker a three-year renewable certificate with a unique ID; the Supreme Court in January 2025 directed states to frame protective measures for domestic workers, and no digital contract or record tool for household employment exists in India or, as far as the search found, anywhere ([IndiaLaw](https://www.indialaw.in/blog/labour/karnataka-domestic-workers-bill-2025/); [Citizen Matters](https://citizenmatters.in/a-step-towards-dignity-karnatakas-draft-domestic-workers-bill-explained/); [Shankar IAS](https://www.shankariasparliament.com/current-affairs/gs-ii/domestic-workers-in-india)). The bill's passage after November 2025 could not be confirmed and unions have concerns about the draft, so this is a possible mandate, not a live one ([TNM explainer](https://www.thenewsminute.com/karnataka/explainer-karnataka-domestic-workers-draft-bill-and-why-unions-are-concerned)). But GigSync's G1 slice, agent-negotiated `JobTerms` with rate, hours and weekly off, hashed and signed by both phones, is a model-format agreement generator aimed at precisely this market (`HLD.md:12-13`; `LLD.md:69-121`). The Domestic Workers' Rights Union's hostility to platform-run "background verification" of workers also tells GigSync what not to become: the record must be of the agreement and payment, held by the worker, not another surveillance layer ([The Wire](https://m.thewire.in/article/labour/fear-sold-as-safety-domestic-workers-groups-slam-hiring-platforms-seek-regulation)).

The third is contact-withholding as a safety primitive. Women on Snabbit and Urban Company who are molested have no recourse but to ask the platform to block the customer; instant-help workers cannot block specific customers themselves; domestic worker unions' demand is "Verify the maalik, not just us" ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/ground-reports/snabbit-urban-company-women-gig-workers-molested-delhi-bengaluru/2877171); [Scroll](https://scroll.in/article/1091102/why-domestic-workers-say-gig-work-platforms-have-let-them-down); [The Third Eye](https://thethirdeyeportal.in/structure/verify-the-maalik-not-just-us/)). GigSync's design releases "number, address and Aadhaar ... only after both phones sign the same terms, and only until the job ends", caps the contact grant at job end or 90 days, lets the worker's own rules auto-decline, and lets a union-governed flag turn the confirm card red without exposing the complainant (`HLD.md:14-15, 228-232`; `LLD.md:582`). None of the agentic-commerce protocols found have a "both sides sign before contact release" step; they assume the merchant is the system of record ([IBM](https://ibm.com/think/topics/agentic-commerce)). This is the one place where the on-device, two-agent architecture is not an engineering preference but the mechanism that makes the safety property possible.

The fourth is the absence of a platform in the middle, which is both the thesis and the moat. Every worker-first model with traction is a platform (Namma Yatri, Bharat Taxi) or a government registry; every startup that raised real money monetises the employer; every global worker-data tool that touched platform data got blocked or starved. GigSync's cloud sees "Usernames, public keys, public profiles, locked envelopes" and nothing else, money moves by plain UPI with no gateway, and the harassment notes and pay disputes never reach a cloud model "even with consent" (`HLD.md:81, 183-185`; `docs/adr/0010-no-cloud-model-in-gigsync.md:20-22`). This is a defensible position precisely because no incumbent can occupy it without abandoning its business model; it is also the reason GigSync cannot copy any incumbent's funding path, which is the problem the recommendation section addresses.

## Build the record first, partner for landing, drop the marketplace

The founders' end-to-end vision and the repository's narrow design can be reconciled, but only in one direction: the record is the spine that makes everything else possible, and "end to end" has to mean "a record that follows the worker across cities, platforms and institutions, plus partners who accept it", not a GigSync-built housing, lending or community product. The evidence for that ordering is that the arrival-and-housing step has no incumbent but also no evidence of a software solution (housing is a bed and a landlord, and Nia.one solves it with real estate and a 5% staffing commission), that peer networks are the least documented step and the one where every community tool found died on community-building, and that the record is the only asset in the whole landscape that no government, platform or startup is building and that gains value precisely when a worker moves ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/startups/how-nia-is-fixing-indias-gig-economics-by-giving-workers-a-home-away-from-home/); [Civic Tech Field Guide](https://civictech.guide/projects/gigbox)).

**First, finish and ship the G1 agreement-and-record slice for off-platform domestic and home-service workers, with one named union, in Bengaluru.** Bengaluru is the only city where a state gig board, a live grievance portal, a fee-funded fund and a draft domestic-workers mandate for written agreements all exist at once, and the Domestic Workers' Rights Union is there ([Chambers](https://chambers.com/articles/digitizing-justice-grievance-redressal-for-platform-based-gig-workers-in-karnataka); [IndiaLaw](https://www.indialaw.in/blog/labour/karnataka-domestic-workers-bill-2025/)). That choice forces two immediate changes to the design: Kannada must join Hindi and Telugu in templates and speech before any pilot, and the model-format fields in Karnataka's draft bill (name, nature of work, daily hours, wages, leave, termination) should become the `JobTerms` schema so that a GigSync agreement is, by construction, the document the state will ask for. The export format should be defined with the people who will receive it: India Labourline's counsellors, Karnataka's IDRC intake (name, address, mobile, description, plus whatever documents the committee asks for), and the Board's dashboard fields of payments, welfare fees and eligible schemes ([Chambers](https://chambers.com/articles/digitizing-justice-grievance-redressal-for-platform-based-gig-workers-in-karnataka); [South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/karnataka/karnataka-platform-based-gig-workers-act-2025-rules-notified-board-to-meet-at-least-once-every-three-months/)). Merge or kill `feat/agent-flow`; replace the `DEV_OTP`, verify envelope signatures, and land the SQLCipher store and sanitizer before anyone real touches the app, since harassment notes are the most sensitive data the product will hold (`docs/adr/0010-no-cloud-model-in-gigsync.md:20-21`). Measure the two unvalidated assumptions in month one as the ADRs themselves demand: WebView on a 4 GB phone, and whether UPI apps honour pre-filled links (`docs/adr/0001-capacitor-one-codebase.md:21`; `docs/adr/0011-upi-qr-payments-no-gateway.md:45-46`).

**Second, make the record do the one "life" job it can do without new infrastructure: registration.** Nine in ten gig workers are outside e-Shram, registration is self-declared and Aadhaar-OTP based, it unlocks ₹2 lakh PMSBY cover and the one-stop scheme window today, and it is the key to every future central and state benefit ([Organiser](https://organiser.org/2026/08/29/377578/bharat/five-years-of-e-shram-31-89-crore-unorganised-workers-join-indias-digital-social-security-net/)). An onboarding step that walks a worker through e-Shram and, in Karnataka, Board registration, stores the UAN and UIN in the ledger, and keeps a per-day engagement count that maps onto the Rules' 90/120-day test costs little and is the most valuable single action in the landscape ([MediaNama](https://www.medianama.com/2026/05/223-social-security-central-rules-2026-gig-workers-90-days-work/)). The same logic applies to a "know your rights" script for opening a bank account on a self-declared address, which the law already permits ([BloombergQuint](https://www.bloombergquint.com/aadhaar/govt-eases-norms-for-change-of-address-on-aadhaar)).

**Third, partner rather than build for housing, peer networks and credit, and let the record be the thing partners consume.** Nia.one has paying demand at the right price point and no record of its members' work history; a union has members, trust and WhatsApp groups and no tooling; a lender has no way to underwrite off-platform domestic work. GigSync's scoped export is the natural interface to all three, and union mode, the only institutional relationship already in the design, is where a "connect me with workers from my region" feature belongs, run by the union under the "collective governance where it is collective" principle rather than by GigSync (`HLD.md:56-57`). The research found no evidence that workers would pay for non-job services (the only price benchmark is Namma Yatri's ₹25 a day) and found that grant dependence killed three Mozilla-funded predecessors, so the funding question has to be answered with partners, not features ([Rest of World](https://restofworld.org/2025/uber-ola-copy-india-zero-commission-ride-hailing-app/)).

**Fourth, drop or defer what the landscape already does better.** The G4 job board competes with Apna, Vahan and Lokal on their home turf with none of their liquidity, and discovery is the one step where a platform in the middle is actually efficient. The G5 ONDC relay for rides and delivery enters a vertical where Namma Yatri, Yatri Sathi and Bharat Taxi already hold the drivers and are bundling benefits. The Bluesky connect-at-onboarding step adds an email and a CAPTCHA to a low-literacy flow for a public profile the pilot does not need; the ADR's own fallback should become the default until the pilot shows demand for a public identity. The `app.gigsync.review` record should go, because it contradicts the no-ratings non-goal and because DWRU has made clear that any worker-rating or verification layer will be read as surveillance ([The Wire](https://m.thewire.in/article/labour/fear-sold-as-safety-domestic-workers-groups-slam-hiring-platforms-seek-regulation)). And the Mozilla Human-Scale AI Incubator application due 20 November 2026 should be reconsidered against the memory note's own finding that a G0 project with zero users fails the deployed-technology gate and that the Fellowship is the better fit; an application that reframes "works with no model at all" as an AI-core product would misdescribe the project (`gigsync_project_summary.md §7`).

The open questions the founders must answer before the pilot are these. Which union, by name, with what MoU, and does it want a desk tool or a members' app? Will any receiving institution (Labourline, a Karnataka IDRC, the Board, an NBFC) accept a both-signed worker-held record as evidence, and in what format; the research found no precedent either way. Will a household employer install an app and sign terms, given that the consumer side has no incentive the design currently names and the HLD itself asks whether consumers need KYC (`HLD.md:303-309`)? Does Karnataka's Domestic Workers Bill pass, and in a form that recognises a phone-signed agreement as the written agreement? How does a worker with no bank account or UPI ID get paid and recorded, since cash entries are allowed but unverifiable? Which languages ship first, given that the pilot city's workers do not speak the two Indian languages planned? Who pays, at what price, and what happens when the grant ends? Is person-to-person UPI outside payment-aggregator rules; counsel has not yet been asked (`docs/adr/0011-upi-qr-payments-no-gateway.md:33-34`). What is the minimum evidence a helpline accepts from a unilateral, uncountersigned receipt? And finally, what is the licence and is the repository public, since neither is settled and both gate every partnership named above (`README.md:182`).

## Conclusion

The research changes the frame in one important way. The founders' "end to end in life" vision and the repository's "agree, withhold contact, record, export" design looked like a scope mismatch; they are better read as a question of sequence. In the Indian landscape of October 2026, every benefit a gig worker can actually claim is gated by a record someone else holds: the platform's activity log for insurance, the aggregator's upload for e-Shram, the state board's registry for the welfare fee, the lender's API link for credit, and nothing at all for the domestic worker paid by a household. The one thing that follows a worker from city to city is what sits on their phone. A record that is countersigned at the moment of agreement, machine-readable by a court's standard, and exportable in the shape an IDRC, a helpline or a board already asks for, is not a smaller ambition than the life vision; it is the only version of that vision no incumbent is positioned to build, and the precondition for every partner who could supply the bed, the loan or the peer group.

The risk is equally clear from the evidence. Worker-data tools do not die from lack of technology; they die when no institution accepts the record, no community forms around it, and the grant runs out. GigSync's next twelve months should therefore be judged not by lines of code but by three external facts: a named union using the record for real cases, one receiving institution accepting it as evidence, and one household employer signing on the other side. Until those exist, the project remains a well-reasoned design in a market that has moved from goodwill to statute while its most vulnerable target users remain outside every register.
