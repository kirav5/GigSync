# Nobody profits without a cut; GigSync must bill institutions

_Research report, 27 October 2026. Synthesised from three research notes (home-service platform economics; institutional buyers and regulation; worker-first and cooperative models) and, where still relevant, the 27 October 2026 landscape report and its six notes. Every figure carries its source and date. "Unverified" marks a figure from a single secondary source, a company claim not checked against filings, or a search snippet whose page could not be fetched. "Derived" marks arithmetic done by the researchers or the writer from cited inputs; the arithmetic is shown. "Assumption" marks a number that exists in no source and is supplied only to make a calculation possible. Rupee crore (Cr) = 10 million; lakh = 100,000. Where a dollar figure is converted, the rate used is ~₹90 per dollar, derived from the notes' own paired figures (₹39.75 Cr ≈ $4.4 M; $2.4 M ≈ ₹21.3 Cr)._

As of 27 October 2026, not one platform in the sample that refuses a per-job cut from the worker has published a profit, and the three instant-help products GigSync's target workers actually use (Urban Company InstaHelp, Snabbit, Pronto) run at _negative_ take rates, paying workers ₹100–135 an hour of logged time while charging households ₹1–₹200 an hour and losing ₹279 per order (InstaHelp, Q2 FY27) or ₹16 for every rupee of revenue (Snabbit, FY26) ([Inc42, 16 Oct 2026](https://inc42.com/buzz/urban-company-q2-loss-widens-over-3x-yoy-to-%E2%82%B9182-5-cr-revenue-up-41/); [Inc42, 16 Oct 2026](https://inc42.com/buzz/snabbit-spent-%E2%82%B916-to-earn-every-rupee-in-operating-revenue-in-fy26/)). The only home-services model that is both profitable and light on worker fees, Yes Madam, gets there by selling products (54% of revenue), not by pricing labour ([StartupTalky, 2026](https://startuptalky.com/news/yes-madam-raises-50-crore-info-edge-growth-funding/)). The only models anywhere near break-even without a cut are the ones where an institution pays for a defined outcome: Haqdarshak (₹40.1 Cr revenue, ₹0.76 Cr loss in FY25, on CSR and government contracts for completed scheme applications) and Nia.one (₹30 Cr ARR from rent plus a 5% staffing commission paid by employers) ([Inc42 financials](https://inc42.com/company/haqdarshak/financials/); [Inc42, 6 May 2026](https://inc42.com/?p=556949)). Worker-subscription ride-hailing has scale but not profit (Namma Yatri lost ₹51.25 Cr on ₹19.8–23.5 Cr in FY25), employer-paid job boards lose money at ₹80–145 Cr of revenue (Apna −₹50 Cr, WorkIndia −₹23 Cr), and every worker-data tool funded by grants or voluntary contributions (Alia, Driver's Seat, Gigbox, Rodeo) is dead or dormant ([Venture Intelligence](https://news.ventureintelligence.com/private-equity/ride-hailing-app-namma-yatri-raises-rs.39.75-cr); [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/apna-narrows-fy25-loss-to-inr-50-cr-even-as-revenue-dips); [Domestic Employers](https://domesticemployers.org/resource/sunsetting-of-alia/)). For GigSync, which by design never routes demand, never takes a cut and never holds a worker database, the arithmetic is unforgiving: at any defensible per-unit price (₹20–₹360 a year per worker, ₹169–₹250 per household or per handover), covering a two-founder team's minimum ₹36 lakh a year (assumption) needs on the order of **10,000–15,000 paying units a year, or one institutional contract of ₹25–30 lakh**, against a pilot sized at 50 workers and 10 newcomers. The candid verdict is that a grant buys 18–30 months, that the only revenue line with a precedent at the right shape is a CSR- or state-paid registration-and-scheme-access service on the Haqdarshak model (price per registration ₹20–₹50, both figures unverified for an app), that partner handover fees and a licensed-partner insurance channel are plausible top-ups worth a few lakh at pilot scale, and that no worker- or household-paid subscription reaches break-even below roughly 12,000 paying users, a number no Indian worker-first product has published.

## Six facts that undermine any GigSync revenue plan before it starts

The weakest point is structural: the two things that make a worker-first platform's subscription work are the two things GigSync refuses to do. Namma Yatri can charge auto drivers ₹25 a day because it delivers rides; 35,000 of 89,573 registered drivers enrolled in the launch week of September 2023, 7,500 of them unprompted, but the company has never since published what share of its 638,000 registered drivers pay, and its FY25 revenue of ₹19.8–23.5 Cr implies about **₹310 a year per registered driver**, under 4% of the ₹9,125 a full-year subscription would cost (derived: ₹19.8 Cr ÷ 638,000; ₹25 × 365) ([Medianama, Aug 2023](https://www.medianama.com/2023/08/223-namma-yatri-drivers-will-pay-subscription-charges-from-september-1/); [Rest of World, 2 May 2025](https://restofworld.org/2025/uber-ola-copy-india-zero-commission-ride-hailing-app/); [Venture Intelligence](https://news.ventureintelligence.com/private-equity/ride-hailing-app-namma-yatri-raises-rs.39.75-cr)). GigSync's VISION.md says the agent "finds work in every mode" but that discovery is by trade, distance and availability on a board GigSync does not rank or monetise, and that it does not try to "out-liquidity Apna or Vahan" (`docs/gigsync/VISION.md §5`). A worker will pay Namma Yatri for rides; the evidence that a worker will pay for a record, a contract template and a landing checklist is zero, and the nearest proxy, Haqdarshak's ₹40–200 per concrete scheme application, is a fee for an outcome, not a subscription ([The Better India](https://thebetterindia.com/244388/pune-haqdarshak-government-schemes-how-to-help-apply-crores-impact-india-gop94)).

Second, the two business models closest to sustainable are not software businesses. Haqdarshak's near-break-even (−1.9% margin on ₹40.1 Cr) sits on **52,000 trained field agents** and 98% B2B/CSR revenue (the 98% figure is from a student project and unverified); Nia.one's ₹30 Cr ARR is rent on about 100 physical studios plus a 5% commission from employers who get staff ([haqdarshak.com](https://haqdarshak.com/); [GrowthX student project, unverified](https://community.growthx.club/proof-of-work/civic---social-organization/haqdarshak/monetisation-project|65c77ae9fafd1a2ca8089cc1); [Inc42, 6 May 2026](https://inc42.com/?p=556949)). Both sell something an institution already budgets for (completed applications; housed, placed workers). A two-founder app with no field force sells neither unless it borrows the field force from a union, and unions have no money: SEWA's documented dues are ₹5 a year, and no fee at all could be found for IFAT, TGPWU, AIGWU or GigWA ([ICMR case study](https://icmrindia.org/Casestudies/catalogue/Leadership%20and%20Entrepreneurship/Self%20Employed%20Womens%20Association%20(SEWA)-Leadership%20and%20Entrepreneurship-Case%20Study.htm); [TGPWU constitution](https://tgpwu.org/?p=101)).

Third, the design may be an "aggregator" in law. The Code on Social Security's definition is "a digital intermediary or a market place for a buyer or user of a service to connect with the seller or the service provider", Karnataka's Act uses "digital intermediary connecting buyers and sellers of goods and services", and GigSync's agent, by its own vision statement, "agrees each job" with the household's agent ([Taxmann, 7 Sep 2026](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/analysis-aggregator-obligations-code-on-social-security/); [Lawrbit](https://www.lawrbit.com/?p=10053); `docs/gigsync/VISION.md §1`). No ruling or case law exists either way. The money at stake is small (Karnataka's 1% fee is capped at ₹1.50 per transaction for professional-service providers), but the obligations are not: Karnataka's Rules require an aggregator to submit a machine-readable worker database and report every join and exit within seven days, and the Central Rules require real-time registration of new workers via API ([SCC Online, 18 Feb 2026](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/02/18/karnataka-government-notifies-gig-workers-welfare-fee-mandatory/); [South First, 31 Mar 2026](https://thesouthfirst.com/karnataka/karnataka-platform-based-gig-workers-act-2025-rules-notified-board-to-meet-at-least-once-every-three-months); [Taxmann](https://www.taxmann.com/post/blog/analysis-aggregator-obligations-code-on-social-security/)). A worker database is the one thing GigSync's vision says it will never hold (`docs/gigsync/VISION.md §6`). Being classed as an aggregator would therefore not cost GigSync revenue; it would break the product.

Fourth, no willingness-to-pay data exists for the thing GigSync sells. The institutional-buyers note searched for an Indian survey of what gig or domestic workers, or households, would pay for safety, verification or a written contract, and found none; the only household-side benchmarks are Urban Company Plus (reported at ₹299 a year by a student teardown and at ₹999–1,999 a year by a templated aggregator site, both unverified and contradictory) and Care.com's $11–39 a month in the United States ([NextLeap teardown, unverified](https://assets.nextleap.app/submissions/DeepikaUrbanCompanyteardown-3d5bcf26-e0a5-4796-a26b-6177bb33c664.pdf); [Subger, unverified](https://subger.com/en/er/service/urban-company); [MoneyPantry](https://moneypantry.com/care-com-review/)). Care.com's consumer subscription revenue fell 10% in Q4 2024 while its employer-paid enterprise segment grew 18% ([IAC Q4 2024 release](https://sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001800227/000180022725000006/ex_991q42024iac-pressrelea.htm)). The household is the shrinking payer even where the model works.

Fifth, the one government price for exactly what GigSync does, helping a worker register, is **₹20 per e-Shram registration**, paid only to CSC Village Level Entrepreneurs and State Seva Kendras (about ₹347 Cr paid to CSC by 31 December 2022), and that channel is closed to other entities; VLEs who charge workers lose their IDs ([labour.gov.in Lok Sabha reply, Mar 2023](https://labour.gov.in/sites/default/files/pib1906432.pdf); [Zee Business, 2021](https://www.zeebiz.com/personal-finance/news-e-shram-portal-vles-should-not-charge-money-for-registration-non-compliance-may-result-in-blocking-of-csc-vle-id-168209)). Karnataka built its welfare-fee verification system with IIIT-Bangalore and the Azim Premji Foundation, not a vendor, and no state board was found to have contracted any NGO or startup per registration or per grievance ([Storyboard18, 28 Jan 2026](https://www.storyboard18.com/how-it-works/karnataka-to-levy-welfare-fee-on-ecommerce-platforms-to-fund-gig-workers-social-security-88392.htm)).

Sixth, grants are the model that killed the predecessors. Driver's Seat Cooperative took $450,000 from Ford (August 2022–July 2024) and $479,700 from Rockefeller (May 2023–June 2025), sold data that cities and researchers used without, on the evidence, paying for it, and was sunsetted into a Princeton lab; Alia, built by the National Domestic Workers Alliance with Google.org money on a voluntary $5-per-cleaning contribution, was discontinued on 9 March 2022 ([Ford Foundation](https://www.fordfoundation.org/work/our-grants/awarded-grants/grants-database/drivers-seat-cooperative-143964/); [Rockefeller Foundation](https://www.rockefellerfoundation.org/grant/drivers-seat-cooperative-2023); [WAO](https://wao.cs.princeton.edu/); [Domestic Employers](https://domesticemployers.org/resource/sunsetting-of-alia/)). The grant is a runway, not a model, and GigSync's own vision lists "who pays" as open question six (`docs/gigsync/VISION.md §10`).

Three things in the table matter more than any single cell. First, the instant-help trio have moved extraction from the worker to the investor and to the worker's _time_: no subscriptions or lead fees, but wages set by fiat and tuned down (Pronto cut new-joiner pay from ₹28,000 to ₹24,000 in November 2025; Snabbit's "upward of ₹40,000" became "₹25,000–30,000" within five months), unpaid waiting of 13–20% of the day by the founder's own estimate, and ₹1,000–2,000 docked per weekend day off ([Scroll, 10 Mar 2026](https://scroll.in/article/1091102/why-domestic-workers-say-gig-work-platforms-have-let-them-down); [Inc42, 8 Sep 2026](https://inc42.com/features/the-ground-reality-for-the-gig-workers-powering-indias-instant-help-boom/); [TechCrunch, 28 May 2025](https://techcrunch.com/2025/05/28/lightspeed-backs-indian-home-services-startup-snabbit-as-the-next-big-consumer-trend/)). On InstaHelp, the advertised ₹150–180 per paid hour against a worker-reported ₹21,000 for 8-hour days implies that only about 55–65% of logged hours are paid (derived: ₹21,000 ÷ ₹165 ≈ 127 paid hours against ~208 logged). Urban Company's own steady-state model (₹200 price, ₹150 payout, ₹50 for everything else) is a 25% take that equals cost, which means the category reaches zero margin only after a 30–40% price rise from today's ₹114 net order value, and management has said orders will fall when that happens ([Medianama, 19 Oct 2026](https://www.medianama.com/2026/10/223-urban-company-investing-instahelp/)). Second, Urban Company's profitable core rests on high-ticket repeat categories (AOV ₹1,505), a ~28% take from professionals, worker-side lines the company does not report as such (a mandatory credit balance of ₹2,500–3,500 that is interest-free working capital from tens of thousands of beauty partners; products sold to professionals at ~23% gross margin, ₹66.2 Cr in Q1 FY27 alone) and a decade of training spend already sunk (~₹524 Cr cumulative) ([Newslaundry, 7 Jul 2025](https://www.newslaundry.com/2025/07/07/long-hours-low-earnings-paying-to-work-the-brutal-life-of-an-urban-company-beautician); [Inc42, 4 Sep 2026](https://inc42.com/features/inside-urban-companys-marketplace-model-what-it-keeps-from-every-booking/); [UC IR FY2024](https://investorrelations.urbancompany.com/announcements-and-highlights/annual-business-summary-fy2024)). None of those conditions exists in domestic work. Third, the agency models that charge only the household (Broomees, BookMyBai) are the closest existing analogue to "no cut from the worker", and the one with public numbers, Broomees, is a ₹6.3 Cr business losing ₹25.8 lakh after four years on a $600,000 seed: a small, honest, nearly-break-even placement agency, not a venture ([Inc42 profile](https://inc42.com/company/broomees/financials/)).

## Only institution-paid outcomes and thin layers on worker-owned businesses survive

Sorting the full sample by who pays gives a clean result. Where a household pays a voluntary add-on, the model died (Alia, $5 per cleaning, discontinued 9 March 2022; the Aspen fellow quoted at launch had already said "a mandatory system would be better") ([Star Tribune](https://www.startribune.com/a-new-app-gives-paid-time-off-to-the-original-gig-workers/504185312/); [Domestic Employers](https://domesticemployers.org/resource/sunsetting-of-alia/)). Where a household pays a compulsory add-on priced into the invoice, the model lives but is a commission by another name: Hilfr's customer pays about DKK 429 an hour of which 22% is "platform, insurance and operations", 58% goes to the cleaner and 20% is VAT; TaskRabbit charges the client a Trust & Support fee plus a service fee of roughly 22.5% combined (unverified) on top of the Tasker's rate ([hilfr.dk](https://hilfr.dk/en); [TaskRabbit support](https://support.taskrabbit.com/hc/articles/204940570); [rapidevelopers, unverified](https://rapidevelopers.com/clone/taskrabbit)). The worker's rate is protected, which is a real difference for the worker, but the load on the transaction is the same as a commission and the platform is still in the middle of the money, which GigSync's "not a bank" rule forbids (`docs/gigsync/VISION.md §6`).

Where the worker pays a flat fee, scale is possible but profit has not been shown anywhere. Namma Yatri lost ₹51.25 Cr on ₹19.8 Cr in FY25, raised a ₹39.75 Cr bridge at a flat ~₹500 Cr valuation in 2026, and its co-founder claims profit per ride "in three of the five states" while the company as a whole does not break even ([Venture Intelligence](https://news.ventureintelligence.com/private-equity/ride-hailing-app-namma-yatri-raises-rs.39.75-cr); [Rest of World, 2 May 2025](https://restofworld.org/2025/uber-ola-copy-india-zero-commission-ride-hailing-app/)). Its government-run and cooperative clones (Yatri Sathi at ₹10 a ride, Kerala Savaari, Bharat Taxi at ₹18–30 a day) publish rides and driver earnings but never cost or revenue, which is itself the signal: they are run as public utilities or cooperative-corpus projects, not businesses expected to cover costs, and a West Bengal minister said at Yatri Sathi's launch that the state "will make no profit" ([Millennium Post, Jun 2025](https://www.millenniumpost.in/bengal/cab-drivers-earnings-via-yatri-sathi-cross-rs-300-cr-since-inception-in-2023-615022); [Telegraph via obeta.ttef.in, unverified](https://obeta.ttef.in/west-bengal/mamata-banerjee-launches-yatri-sathi-app-to-enable-commuters-to-get-taxi-service/cid/1973810)). Kerala Savaari's first version lost drivers to better-paying private apps and passengers uninstalled; the 2.0 relaunch in November 2025 answered with a central PSU (ITI Ltd) as technology partner rather than a new revenue source ([Platform.coop repost of Mathrubhumi, 10 Apr 2023](https://resources.platform.coop/resources/project-kerala-savaari-facing-a-bumpy-ride-drivers-leave-passengers-uninstall-app); [UNI India, Nov 2025](https://www.uniindia.com/iti-joins-kerala-govt-to-launch-kerala-savaari-2-0/business-economy/news/3640822.html)). Uber, Ola and Rapido have since copied the subscription for autos, so the model's moat lasted about two years ([Rest of World](https://restofworld.org/2025/uber-ola-copy-india-zero-commission-ride-hailing-app/)).

Where an employer pays for supply, revenue is real and the loss is structural. Apna's recruitment revenue grew 61% to ₹57.1 Cr in FY25 while total revenue dipped and the loss was ₹50.1 Cr; WorkIndia lost ₹23.06 Cr on ₹78.7 Cr; Vahan, which places riders for Zomato and Swiggy, lost ₹11.98 Cr on ₹26.93 Cr in FY24 ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/apna-narrows-fy25-loss-to-inr-50-cr-even-as-revenue-dips); [Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/news/workindia-raises-rs-97-cr-in-series-b-led-by-aavishkaar-capital-10922965); [TheKredible](https://thekredible.com/company/vahan/overview)). These are liquidity businesses; they lose money to buy both sides of the market, which GigSync has ruled out.

Where an institution pays for a defined outcome, the model is closest to self-funding. Haqdarshak earned ₹40.1 Cr in FY25 against ₹40.8 Cr of expenses, a −1.9% margin, selling completed scheme applications, data and SaaS to "government, CSR and enterprise clients"; its 7.6 million families reached and ₹18,800 Cr of benefits unlocked are the deliverables the buyers pay for ([Inc42 financials](https://inc42.com/company/haqdarshak/financials/); [haqdarshak.com](https://haqdarshak.com/)). Nia.one's ₹30 Cr ARR is rent plus a 5% commission from employers who receive a housed, fed, placed worker ([Inc42, 6 May 2026](https://inc42.com/?p=556949)). Care.com's profitable segment is the one where employers buy back-up care for staff ([IAC Q4 2024](https://sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/0001800227/000180022725000006/ex_991q42024iac-pressrelea.htm)). SMart in Belgium takes 6.5% of every member invoice and runs members as salaried employees, with 34,000 members and 203,000 contracts in 2022 and a positive current result in 2024; Up & Go in New York takes 5% (voted to 8% in 2019) on top of worker-cooperative pricing and projected self-sufficiency "after roughly 23,000 jobs" ([SMart chiffres 2022](https://smartbe.be/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/20230612_SmartBE-Atelier-Chiffres.pdf); [Rapport de gestion 2025](https://new.smartbe.be/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Rapport-de-gestion.pdf); [MIT Solve](https://solve.mit.edu/solutions/3028)). Both of the latter are thin layers on a worker-owned business whose clients pay full price; both take a percentage of invoiced value, which is a cut.

Where a grant or voluntary contribution pays, the model dies when the money stops. Driver's Seat (≈ $930,000 of Ford and Rockefeller grants between August 2022 and June 2025) is sunsetted; Gigbox, a Mozilla Technology Fund project, has no post-2024 status; Rodeo in the UK raised a $5 M seed in April 2023, was cut off from Deliveroo's rider-app data by a "security update" in March 2023, and its domain did not resolve on 27 October 2026; Worker Info Exchange and Fairwork survive by design as grant-funded research and litigation bodies that take no platform money ([Ford Foundation](https://www.fordfoundation.org/work/our-grants/awarded-grants/grants-database/drivers-seat-cooperative-143964/); [Rockefeller Foundation](https://www.rockefellerfoundation.org/grant/drivers-seat-cooperative-2023); [Mozilla Foundation](https://www.mozillafoundation.org/en/blog/giving-gig-workers-the-transparency-they-deserve/); [UKTN, 21 Mar 2023](https://www.uktech.news/mobility/deliveroo-blocks-rodeo-20230321); [WIE funding](https://www.workerinfoexchange.org/funding); [Fairwork credits](https://fair-work.shorthandstories.com/credits-and-funding/)). Gram Vaani's founder put it most plainly after a decade of pivots: "the ideal model still remains elusive" ([arXiv 1907.06360](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.06360.pdf)).

## Regulation prices every candidate line, and classification is the real risk

The institutional-buyers note mapped each regime onto a non-intermediary app that records jobs and never touches money. The headline result is that GigSync sits outside the three heaviest regimes (RBI payment-aggregator authorisation, TDS on e-commerce, state welfare fees) precisely because each hinges on facilitating a transaction or handling funds, and GigSync does neither by design. But the comfort is partial: the explicit RBI carve-out language for person-to-person UPI was not located in any source and counsel has not been asked (`docs/gigsync/VISION.md §10`), and the "connect" test for aggregator status is a function test that GigSync's job-agreement feature arguably meets.

The inference that should govern the business model is the mirror of the regulatory one: the regimes that would tax GigSync are the regimes that would also force it to become what it refuses to be. Every rupee of per-transaction fee, TDS or welfare levy comes bundled with a duty to hold money, hold a worker database or submit worker records to the state. "Not a platform, not a bank, not a database" is therefore not only a product stance but the entire regulatory moat, and any revenue line that requires GigSync to sit in the transaction (a household fee collected per job, an escrow, a wallet, a "verified worker" marketplace) forfeits it.

## Ten candidate revenue lines, with the evidence for and against each

The brief asked for ten lines. They are ranked here from best-evidenced to worst, with the price evidence that exists and the price assumption used in the next section where none exists.

**1. Registration and scheme-access service paid by CSR or government (the Haqdarshak model).** For: it is the only line in the sample where an Indian buyer with a budget has paid, at scale, for the exact deliverable GigSync's landing step produces (a worker registered on e-Shram and a state board, counted against the 90/120-day test). Haqdarshak's ₹40.1 Cr FY25 revenue is predominantly CSR and government programme stipends; a pay-per-outcome coalition (Haqdarshak, Dvara Money and two others) targeted 11 million workers into social security in three years from November 2021; the Centre pays ₹20 per e-Shram registration to CSCs (₹347 Cr by December 2022); Karnataka's Board has a projected ₹250–300 Cr a year with admin capped at 5% and a statutory duty to run schemes for registered workers ([Inc42 financials](https://inc42.com/company/haqdarshak/financials/); [The Hindu via Webjosh, Nov 2021](https://www.webjosh.com/article/private-entities-to-bring-11-million-workers-under-social-security-net-1638168755-59511); [labour.gov.in](https://labour.gov.in/sites/default/files/pib1906432.pdf); [Storyboard18, 28 Jan 2026](https://www.storyboard18.com/how-it-works/karnataka-to-levy-welfare-fee-on-ecommerce-platforms-to-fund-gig-workers-social-security-88392.htm)). Nine in ten gig workers are outside e-Shram (~8.58 lakh gig registrations in January 2026 against NITI's 1.27 crore estimate), so the deliverable is scarce ([SuperKalam/The Hindu, 2 Sep 2026](https://superkalam.com/current-affairs/02-09-2026/majority-of-india-s-gig-workers-remain-out-of-govt-s-reach-pg9-82445054-7e1d-4709-97f8-0579fb712b59)). Against: the ₹20 channel is closed to non-CSC entities; no state board has contracted an app or NGO per registration; Karnataka builds in-house with IIIT-B and the Azim Premji Foundation; Haqdarshak's per-outcome contract prices are not public and its model needs 52,000 agents; no actual Karnataka fee collection has been published and the fee is in a court registry. Price evidence: ₹20 (government to CSC, closed channel), ~₹50 per application (Haqdarshak citizen charge, unverified). Assumption for the arithmetic: ₹20–₹50 per completed registration, or a programme contract of ₹25–50 lakh (assumption, no precedent for an app vendor).

**2. Placement or handover fee from staffing or housing partners.** For: this is the best-evidenced third-party fee in India. Nia.one charges employers 5% on staffing placements; Apna's recruitment revenue was ₹57.1 Cr in FY25 with pay-per-hire recognised after a retention period; Vahan's ₹26.93 Cr FY24 revenue is placement fees from Zomato and Swiggy ([Inc42, 6 May 2026](https://inc42.com/?p=556949); [Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/apna-narrows-fy25-loss-to-inr-50-cr-even-as-revenue-dips); [TheKredible](https://thekredible.com/company/vahan/overview)). GigSync's vision explicitly names Nia.one as "GigSync's natural housing partner" and a lender as "a balance sheet" (`docs/gigsync/VISION.md §7`). Against: no evidence that any housing provider or skilling centre pays listing or referral fees to a worker app in India; placement fees are paid for _supply_, and principle 7 of the vision ("a partner receives a worker and a consent, never a lead list. GigSync introduces; it does not sell") forbids steering, which is how Vahan and Apna earn; a fee per landed worker creates exactly the incentive to push workers toward the paying partner. Price evidence: 5% of a placement (Nia.one); Apna ₹2,500–6,000 per post (unverified); Vahan's per-placement price is not public and the derived "low hundreds" is on mismatched periods and must not be cited as a price. Assumption for the arithmetic: ₹250 per landed worker (assumption; one-third of Nia.one's 5% on a ₹15,000 placement, which is ₹750).

**3. Insurance distribution via a licensed intermediary.** For: embedded insurance in a worker app exists (Covrzy × Namma Yatri, June 2025, phase 1 ~3,000 Bengaluru drivers, target 50,000 in 12–18 months; hospitalisation up to ₹3 lakh); Onsurity sells SME and gig health plans from ₹49 per employee per month (unverified) on a subscription model with "marginal commission income"; the Centre is drafting a life and accident scheme for gig workers; Urban Company's own partner cover shows the demand (claims ₹6.29 Cr in FY24) ([Digital Terminal](https://digitalterminal.in/startup/insurtech-startup-covrzy-to-provide-embedded-health-insurance-to-namma-yatri-drivers); [Lapaas, unverified](https://voice.lapaas.com/startup/onsurity/business-model/); [Business Standard, Mar 2026](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/insurance/centre-drafting-insurance-scheme-for-gig-workers-with-life-accident-cover-126031300772_1.html); [UC IR FY2024](https://investorrelations.urbancompany.com/announcements-and-highlights/annual-business-summary-fy2024)). Against: GigSync cannot be paid commission unless it is a POSP, corporate agent or broker, or a sub-channel of one; no commission rate paid to Covrzy, Namma Yatri or any gig distributor is public; the cheapest cover a worker actually gets through e-Shram, PMSBY, costs ₹20 a year, leaving nothing to distribute; more than 80% of surveyed workers found accident-claims hard, so a distributor that cannot help with claims earns resentment, not renewals ([The Wire](https://thewire.in/labour/gig-workers-in-india-face-risks-extreme-weather-accidents)). Price evidence: none for commission. Assumption: 15% of a ₹588-a-year premium (₹49 × 12, unverified price) = ₹88 per insured worker per year, inside IRDAI's 30% EoM ceiling.

**4. Grants.** For: Mozilla's Data Futures Lab made awards up to $100,000 with gig work an eligible domain and funded Indian projects; Mozilla Ventures invested in Rodeo; Omidyar Network India ran a ₹7.5 Cr rapid-response fund for gig-worker tech in 2020; Ford's Future of Work(ers) programme spends ~$37 M a year globally and profiles IFAT; the Azim Premji Foundation is already paying for gig-worker payment-verification infrastructure in Bengaluru; SAMRIDH disbursed ₹93.75 Cr to 241 startups over five years, about ₹39 lakh each (derived) ([Mozilla Foundation](https://www.mozillafoundation.org/en/blog/data-futures-lab-invites-applications-from-organisations-collecting-data-for-the-public-benefit/); [Inc42, 2020](https://inc42.com/buzz/omidyar-commits-1-mn-for-tech-solutions-to-support-gig-workers-daily-wagers); [Ford Foundation, Sep 2025](https://www.fordfoundation.org/news-and-stories/big-ideas/champions-of-fair-labor-ensuring-worker-rights-and-economic-justice/); [Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/government-schemes/centre-disburses-93-75-crore-to-241-startups-under-samridh-scheme-over-5-years)). Against: no 2025–26 India gig-worker tech call was found from any of them; every grant-funded worker-data predecessor died within two to four years of the grant; the project's own memory note found it fails Mozilla's deployed-technology gate for the Human-Scale AI Incubator due 20 November 2026 (see the 27 October 2026 landscape report). Price evidence: $100,000 ≈ ₹90 lakh (derived rate); Driver's Seat ≈ $930,000 over three years.

**5. Worker subscription.** For: Namma Yatri proved Indian gig workers will pay a flat ₹25 a day where the platform delivers demand; Haqdarshak's citizens pay ₹40–200 per application; a VoxDev-cited urban India study found workers willing to trade wages for job security ([Medianama, Aug 2023](https://www.medianama.com/2023/08/223-namma-yatri-drivers-will-pay-subscription-charges-from-september-1/); [The Better India](https://thebetterindia.com/244388/pune-haqdarshak-government-schemes-how-to-help-apply-crores-impact-india-gop94); [VoxDev](https://www.voxdev.org/topic/job-security-or-flexibility)). Against: GigSync delivers no demand; Namma Yatri's revenue per registered driver is ~₹310 a year and it still loses ₹51 Cr; 40% of gig workers earn under ₹15,000 a month (Economic Survey 2025-26, via the landscape report); Urban Company's worker subscriptions (~₹1,000 per 10-day plan) are the single most protested feature in the sector, and GigSync's pitch to unions is that it is not that ([Newslaundry, 7 Jul 2025](https://www.newslaundry.com/2025/07/07/long-hours-low-earnings-paying-to-work-the-brutal-life-of-an-urban-company-beautician); [Inc42, 22 Dec 2021](https://inc42.com/buzz/urban-company-sues-partners-protesting-against-its-subscription-system-calls-protests-illegal-violent-prone/)). If charged at all it should be per concrete outcome (an evidence pack filed, an export to a lender), on Haqdarshak's pattern, not per day. Price evidence: ₹25/day (ride-hailing, not comparable), ₹40–200 per outcome (Haqdarshak). Assumption: ₹30 a month or ₹50 per outcome, optional.

**6. Household subscription.** For: AuthBridge finds ~20% of gig workers fail address verification, so households have a verification pain; Urban Company's memberships earned ₹98 Cr in FY25; BookMyBai and Broomees prove households pay a placement agency one month's salary or a monthly membership for a helper ([Tribune](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/business/20-gig-workers-fail-address-verification-raises-concern-of-customer-safety-report/amp); [Entrackr, 18 Jun 2025](https://entrackr.com/fintrackr/urban-company-posts-rs-1144-cr-revenue-and-rs-285-cr-pbt-in-fy25-9373371); [BookMyBai charges](https://www.bookmybai.com/our-charges.aspx)). Against: no WTP number exists for an independent, worker-held credential; the household's incentive to install an app and sign terms is open question three in the vision; Alia's voluntary household contribution failed; a household fee collected per job risks the e-commerce-entity and ECO classifications above; domestic-worker unions read platform verification of _workers_ as surveillance ("Verify the maalik, not just us"), so the household must be the one verified, which no household has shown it will pay for ([The Third Eye](https://thethirdeyeportal.in/structure/verify-the-maalik-not-just-us/)). Price evidence: ₹299/yr (NextLeap, unverified) to ₹999–1,999/yr (Subger, unverified) for UC Plus; $11–39/month Care.com. Assumption: ₹199 a year.

**7. Union-paid.** For: unions are the natural channel and TGPWU helped 62% of surveyed members with registration, scholarships and claims; TGPWU's constitution allows funding by "grants, aids, contributions, donations and loans" ([TGPWU](https://tgpwu.org/?p=101)). Against: dues are ₹5 a year at SEWA and unpublished everywhere else; a union of 10,000 active members has at most a few lakh rupees a year in dues; no union tooling budget was found. Verdict: not a payer; a channel through which a funder pays. Price evidence: none.

**8. Union desk SaaS.** Same evidence as line 7. The desk is a cost GigSync carries to get distribution, or a line item in a foundation grant to the union. Kerala Savaari 2.0 shows the one way "union tooling" gets paid: the Kerala Motor Workers Welfare Fund Board operates it and a central PSU (ITI Ltd) builds it, with no contract value disclosed ([UNI India](https://www.uniindia.com/iti-joins-kerala-govt-to-launch-kerala-savaari-2-0/business-economy/news/3640822.html)).

**9. Partner listing fee.** For: a registry of vouched partners (hostels, helplines, skilling bodies, lenders) is in the vision, and Nia.one monetises its own margin on rent, food and groceries ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/?p=556949)). Against: no evidence anywhere in India of a housing or skilling provider paying a worker app to be listed; a listing fee creates the same steering conflict as line 2 and makes the "vouched" label purchasable, which destroys the thing the worker is trusting. Price evidence: none. Not modelled.

**10. Consented income-data fees via Account Aggregator.** For: the RBIH × Vivifi pilot (December 2024) underwrites gig loans on platform job history with consent and is meant to join ULI, which had disbursed ₹27,000 Cr by 6 December 2024; a countersigned off-platform record is exactly the alternative data lenders lack ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/rbi-innovation-hub-pilots-unsecured-loan-offerings-for-gig-workers)). Against: AA fees flow from the lender to the AA and the data provider, neither of which an app can be; DPDP's purpose limitation forbids repurposing; RBI digital-lending rules constrain referral fees; no lender yet accepts a worker-held record. Verdict: a _feature_ that makes the worker's record valuable to the worker, not a revenue line. Pronto Verified, which sells de-identified head-cam footage of cleaning to US AI labs and reportedly expects AI data to be 90% of revenue in five years, is the cautionary opposite: monetising the work itself, with a privacy backlash that made Urban Company and Snabbit distance themselves ([Inc42, May 2026](https://inc42.com/buzz/how-prontos-physical-ai-pilot-raised-questions-around-consent-surveillance/); [Business Today, 25 May 2026](https://www.businesstoday.in/technology/startups/story/pronto-defends-ai-camera-pilot-after-privacy-backlash-rivals-urban-company-snabbit-distance-themselves-533136-2026-05-25)).

What makes any of these "better for workers than theirs" is simple to state and hard to keep: none touches the worker's rate, none ranks, none holds the money, and the only worker-paid line is optional and per outcome. The sector's extraction is now mostly invisible (unpaid waiting, docked leave, credit floats, product markups), so the test for GigSync is not "does the worker pay" but "does anything GigSync earns depend on the worker earning less, waiting more, or being steered". Lines 1, 3 and 4 pass that test; line 2 passes only under principle 7; lines 5 and 6 pass if optional; lines 9 and 10 fail it.

## Break-even for two founders needs 10,000 paying units or one contract

No source gives a cost base for a two-founder Indian startup, so the following is marked throughout. **Assumption A1:** minimum annual cash cost ₹36 lakh, being two founders at ₹1 lakh a month each (₹24 lakh) plus ₹12 lakh for cloud, legal counsel (the P2P-UPI and aggregator questions alone), compliance, travel and field costs. **Assumption A2 (lean):** ₹24 lakh if the founders draw ₹50,000 a month each. **Assumption A3:** GST at 18% is absorbed in the price for any line paid by a worker or household (net = price ÷ 1.18), because the payers are price-sensitive; it is charged on top for institutional contracts. **Assumption A4:** no field staff, no union payments, no marketing spend; Urban Company spent ₹207 Cr on ads in FY25 and Snabbit ₹48.7 Cr in FY26 to acquire households, which GigSync cannot and should not match ([Entrackr](https://entrackr.com/fintrackr/urban-company-posts-rs-1144-cr-revenue-and-rs-285-cr-pbt-in-fy25-9373371); [Inc42, 16 Oct 2026](https://inc42.com/buzz/snabbit-spent-%E2%82%B916-to-earn-every-rupee-in-operating-revenue-in-fy26/)).

Read across the rows, the pattern is that every per-unit line sits in the ₹20–₹305 band and so needs 10,000–20,000 paying units a year to carry even a lean team, while the pilot produces under ₹30,000 a year across all lines combined. For calibration, Namma Yatri reached ~₹310 of revenue per registered driver per year only after four years, ~$11 M of venture money and 638,000 registrations, and Driver's Seat never reached a paying unit at all ([Venture Intelligence](https://news.ventureintelligence.com/private-equity/ride-hailing-app-namma-yatri-raises-rs.39.75-cr); [Entrackr, Jul 2024](https://entrackr.com/2024/07/namma-yatri-raises-11-mn-from-blume-antler-google-and-others)). If GigSync converts 5–20% of registered workers to a paid plan (assumption; Namma Yatri's revenue per registered driver equals about 3–4% of a full-year subscription, derived), covering ₹36 lakh by worker subscription alone needs **60,000–240,000 registered workers**, which is 1,200–4,800 times the pilot and between 7% and 28% of the ~8.58 lakh gig workers registered on e-Shram in January 2026 (derived) ([SuperKalam/The Hindu](https://superkalam.com/current-affairs/02-09-2026/majority-of-india-s-gig-workers-remain-out-of-govt-s-reach-pg9-82445054-7e1d-4709-97f8-0579fb712b59)).

The combination that could plausibly reach break-even, with every input marked, is this. **Year 1 (pilot, Bengaluru, one union):** a single grant of ₹50–90 lakh (Mozilla DFL ceiling or a SAMRIDH-class award) covers costs; revenue from all lines is under ₹1 lakh; the deliverable is the three external facts the landscape report named (a union filing real cases, one institution accepting the record, households signing). **Year 2:** one CSR or state-board programme contract for registration and scheme access at ₹25–30 lakh (assumption; the shape is Haqdarshak's, the price has no precedent for an app and must be negotiated from the ₹20-per-registration government benchmark and the ₹50-per-application citizen benchmark, i.e. 50,000–150,000 registrations' worth of value in a state where ~5 lakh gig workers are mostly unregistered), plus 2,000 landed workers through housing and lending partners at ₹212 net (≈ ₹4.2 lakh), plus 5,000 workers insured through a licensed partner at ₹88 (≈ ₹4.4 lakh), plus 1,000 optional per-outcome worker payments at ₹50 (≈ ₹42,000 net). That sums to roughly **₹34–39 lakh**, at or just above A1, and it requires about **5,000–10,000 active workers and one institutional buyer**, which is 100–200 times the pilot's roster within about 18 months of it. Without the institutional contract, the same user base yields ₹9–10 lakh, a quarter of A1 and well under A2.

Two honest caveats sit on top of that arithmetic. The institution-paid model is near break-even for Haqdarshak only at ₹40 Cr of revenue and with 52,000 agents, which means the outcome is produced by people in the field, not by an app; GigSync's version works only if the union's stewards are the field force and the contract pays for their time, which turns "union-paid" into "union-paid-by-the-contract" and needs an MoU no one has yet signed. And every line in the combination except the grant depends on a buyer who does not yet exist in the evidence: no state board has contracted an app, no housing partner has paid a worker app, no insurer's commission to a gig channel is public, and no lender accepts a worker-held record. The arithmetic shows the shape of break-even; it does not show that anyone will pay.

## Conclusion

The research shifts the revenue question from "which fee" to "which buyer". Every model in the sample that takes money from the transaction (commission, subscription, add-on) either loses money or is a cut by another name, and every model that monetises the worker's data or time either died or is a privacy scandal in progress. The only payers growing and solvent are institutions buying outcomes: CSR and government buying completed registrations, employers buying housed and placed workers, insurers paying licensed channels. That is also, not coincidentally, the only set of payers whose money does not require GigSync to stand in the transaction, hold the money or keep a worker database, which is to say the only set compatible with the regulatory position that protects the product. The design choice "GigSync introduces; it does not sell" is therefore not a constraint on revenue; it is the revenue model, provided the thing introduced (a registered worker with a countersigned record and a consent) is something an institution will pay for per unit.

What follows for the founders is that the pilot's success metrics and the business model's first proof are the same three facts, and the revenue plan should be written as a procurement plan. The first external buyer to approach is not a household or a worker but whichever body in Karnataka is already spending on registration (the Board, with its 5% admin cap and statutory scheme duty, or a CSR funder in the Haqdarshak coalition's mould), with a price per registered, day-counted worker anchored between ₹20 and ₹50 and a contract shape anchored on a programme stipend. The grant buys the eighteen months needed to produce the evidence that buyer will ask for. If, at the end of those months, no institution has paid for a single registration and no partner for a single handover, the honest conclusion will be that GigSync is a public good like Yatri Sathi or Fairwork, to be funded as one, rather than a company, and the founders should decide now which of those two they are building.
