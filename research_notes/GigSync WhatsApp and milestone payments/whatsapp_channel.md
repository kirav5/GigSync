# WhatsApp as a channel for a worker-first gig app in India (as of 28 Oct 2026)

Scope: cost, privacy and control risk of adding a WhatsApp bot (via WhatsApp Business Platform / Cloud API) to GigSync, for (a) workers who prefer WhatsApp, (b) households who will not install an app, (c) notifications and links. Research done 28 Oct 2026; every rate below is as published on the cited page at that date. "Unverified" = found only in a secondary/vendor source, not confirmed on a Meta page.

## Key Question 1: Pricing, free windows, templates, BSP markups, opt-in rules (India, 2026)

### Takeaway

Meta bills per delivered business-to-user message, by category and recipient country. For India the official Meta list rates (per BSP pass-through of Meta's INR rate card, Oct 2026) are roughly ₹0.86 marketing, ₹0.115 utility, ₹0.115 authentication, and from 1 Oct 2026 ₹0.115 per service (free-form) message beyond 1,000 free per phone number per month. Inbound user messages are always free. The practical cost driver for GigSync is not Meta's rate but the BSP markup and subscription (₹0–2,500+/month) and the per-template approval friction.

### Cited Findings

**Model and dated changes (Meta primary source)**
- Per-message pricing replaced conversation-based pricing on 1 Jul 2025; only business-to-user messages are charged, and only when delivered; the user messaging the business is free — [Meta pricing doc](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/pricing.md) (read 28 Oct 2026).
- Categories: marketing, utility, authentication (template messages, can be sent any time); service (non-template, only inside the customer-service window); Meta Business Agent (AI) messages charged per token at US$2.00 per 1M tokens, no free tier — same source.
- 24-hour customer service window (CSW): opens when a user messages or calls the business, resets with each new user message; inside it the business can send free-form (non-template) messages; outside it only templates — same source.
- Free entry point (FEP): when a user messages from a Click-to-WhatsApp ad (Android/iOS only) and the business replies inside the CSW, a window of up to 7 days opens in which marketing, utility, authentication and service messages are not charged — same source. (Respond.io adds that a Facebook-Page WhatsApp-button FEP is 72 h and ad FEPs were extended from 72 h to 7 days — [respond.io](https://respond.io/blog/whatsapp-pricing-change-2026), unverified against Meta.)
- Service messages: each business phone number gets 1,000 free service messages per month, no roll-over; from 1 Oct 2026 the 1,001st onward is charged; with no payment method on file, service messages are not delivered once the free tier is used; eligible government/non-profit organisations keep free service messages through 31 Dec 2027 — Meta pricing doc.
- Utility messages inside an open CSW were free from 1 Jul 2025 but are charged from 1 Oct 2026; utility inside an FEP window stays free — Meta pricing doc.
- Volume tiers exist for utility and authentication only (lower rates at higher monthly volume, aggregated across the business portfolio, reset monthly) — Meta pricing doc.
- India-specific dated changes on Meta's page: 1 Jan 2026 India marketing rate raised; 1 Jan 2026 billing localisation to INR launched for India, and non-INR accounts must migrate by 31 Dec 2026 or messages stop from 1 Jan 2027; 1 Apr 2026 India authentication-international rate raised; India not listed among the 1 Oct 2026 rate changes — Meta pricing doc.
- Meta's pricing page gives no INR figures inline; rates are in downloadable INR/USD rate cards — Meta pricing doc. (I could not fetch the rate card itself; business.whatsapp.com/products/platform-pricing redirected and was not fetched.)

**Opt-in, bot-initiated messages, marketing limits**
- Business-initiated messages outside the CSW must use a Meta-approved template; implied opt-in is not valid; businesses must offer opt-out — [Infobip opt-in guide](https://www.infobip.com/en/blog/how-to-collect-whatsapp-business-opt-ins); [Rasayel policy summary](https://learn.rasayel.io/en/books/whatsapp/whatsapp-business/whatsapp-business-messaging-policy) (secondary).
- Since Nov 2024 the opt-in no longer has to name WhatsApp specifically; a general messaging opt-in is accepted — [CM.com](https://www.cm.com/blog/whatsapp-updates-opt-in-and-quality-rating-policy/) (unverified against Meta policy text).
- India per-user marketing cap: since Feb 2024 Meta limits marketing templates that would open a new conversation to a small number per user per 24 h (sources say two) unless the user responds; utility and authentication templates are exempt — [Exotel guide](https://support.exotel.com/support/solutions/articles/3000125954-whatsapp-s-new-marketing-message-limits-policy-change-detailed-guide); Meta's Feb 2026 doc wording (via mirror) says WhatsApp "may limit" marketing templates where users are unlikely to engage — [chatarchitect mirror of Meta doc](https://support.chatarchitect.com/books/meta-whatsapp/page/per-user-marketing-template-message-limits-developer-documentation).
- Templates with low read rates can be auto-paused; un-pause via WhatsApp Manager or API — [MoEngage](https://www.moengage.com/blog/whatsapps-latest-deliverability-rules/) (secondary).
- Template review takes up to 24 h; rejections for policy violations; low-quality templates get paused then disabled — [Messangi quality doc](https://support.messangi.com/hc/en-us/articles/39008595996187-How-WhatsApp-Business-Manages-Quality) (secondary).
- Meta paused marketing templates to US (+1) numbers from 1 Apr 2025; not applicable to India — [Zoho](https://help.zoho.com/portal/en/kb/marketing-automation-2-0/faqs/marketing-campaigns/whatsapp/articles/what-is-the-pause-on-whatsapp-marketing-messages-for-us-numbers).

### Inferences

- GigSync's traffic is almost entirely utility-shaped (job offer, counter-offer, terms, reminders) plus free-form replies inside the 24 h window. At ₹0.115–0.145 per utility template and ₹0 for inbound, a negotiation of ~10 bot-sent messages per job costs about ₹1.2–1.5 per job if every message is a template, and near ₹0 if the household replies (opening the CSW) and the bot answers with service messages within the 1,000/number/month free tier. Beyond 1,000 service messages per number per month (from 1 Oct 2026), each costs ~₹0.115.
- Rough monthly bill for 1,000 active jobs/month: 10,000 messages × ₹0.13 ≈ ₹1,300 + BSP subscription ₹0–2,500. Direct Cloud API integration removes the markup but needs the team to run webhooks, template management and Meta Business verification.
- Marketing templates (₹0.86–1.09, capped per user per day in India, auto-paused on low read rate) are the wrong category for GigSync; anything that reads as promotional risks re-categorisation and cost ~7× utility.
- Meta Business Agent (AI replies) is charged per token and would route household messages through Meta's model, which is incompatible with GigSync's "no cloud model" promise.

## Key Question 2: Privacy model, Cloud API storage, DPDP, WhatsApp Flows

### Takeaway

Business-to-user WhatsApp is not end-to-end encrypted to the business: Meta's Cloud API holds the keys, decrypts every message, retains it up to 30 days, and forwards plaintext to the business (and its BSP). Any job terms, addresses or phone numbers negotiated in chat are readable by Meta (as processor), the BSP and GigSync's server, which breaks the "private data never leaves the phone readable" promise for that channel. WhatsApp Flows with an encrypted endpoint narrow this for form data but do not change it for chat.

### Cited Findings

- "Cloud API manages the encryption/decryption keys on behalf of the business"; messages travel encrypted between the user and Cloud API; "Cloud API decrypts the message and forwards it to the business" — [Meta data privacy & security](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/data-privacy-and-security) (read 28 Oct 2026).
- Messages have a maximum retention of 30 days (for retransmission), encrypted at rest; user identifiers deleted within 30 days of last status update unless directed otherwise — same source.
- Meta acts as data processor/service provider for the business under the Meta Hosting Terms for Cloud API; sub-processors are listed in the WhatsApp Business Data Processing Terms — same source.
- Meta states other parts of Meta do not have access to Cloud API business communications and messages are not used to inform ads — [Meta data privacy doc via search summary](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/data-privacy-and-security); 360dialog describes the same key-management model — [360dialog architecture](https://docs.360dialog.com/docs/resources/architecture-and-security).
- Cloud API Local Storage lets a business opt to store message data in a data centre in a designated country (list on a separate page; India not confirmed) — [Meta local storage](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/local-storage/).
- WhatsApp Flows: data exchanged between a Flow and the business's endpoint is encrypted with a per-request 128-bit AES key that is itself RSA-OAEP-encrypted to a business-held public key (private key stays with the business); the endpoint response is AES-GCM encrypted with the same key — [Meta Flows endpoint guide](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/flows/guides/implementingyourflowendpoint). The doc does not state whether Meta can read the final `response_json`/params forwarded to the messages webhook, and the Flows overview page says nothing about Meta's access — [Meta Flows overview](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/flows).
- DPDP Act: consent must be free, specific, informed and a clear affirmative act (s.6(1)); the business deploying the chatbot is the Data Fiduciary even when a BSP runs it — [SCC Online, 29 Jul 2026](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/07/29/whatsapp-chatbot-opt-in-consent-dpdp-act-compliance/); [Periskope guide](https://periskope.app/blog/how-to-achieve-dpdp-compliance-in-whatsapp-workflows) (vendor).
- Sources conflict on DPDP enforcement timing: one says full enforcement May 2027, another says 2026 — [boei.help](https://boei.help/dpdp-compliant-ai-chatbot/) vs [MyOperator](https://myoperator.com/blog/whatsapp-compliance-india-dpdp-meta-policy-2026) (both vendor; unverified).
- Aadhaar is separately governed by the Aadhaar Act 2016; vendor guidance is to collect at most a masked Aadhaar in chat — [DPDPA.com](https://www.dpdpa.com/blogs/whatsapp_business_dpdpa_compliance_messaging_apps.html) (vendor, unverified).
- Haqdarshak's agents do not enter PII (Aadhaar, bank account) into their app because it is not needed to determine eligibility — [IIMA alumni piece on Haqdarshak](https://wimwian.iima.ac.in/?p=359349).

### Inferences

- Any WhatsApp channel means three parties (Meta Cloud API, BSP if used, GigSync server) see job terms in plaintext for up to 30 days at Meta. GigSync can truthfully say "no cloud model" only if the bot is rule-based and no Meta Business Agent / BSP AI feature is enabled; it cannot say "private data never leaves the phone readable" for WhatsApp traffic.
- The least-leaky design: WhatsApp carries only a short link and a minimal utility notification ("A worker has sent you job terms, open link"); the terms, negotiation, signature and payment happen on a web page served by GigSync (or peer-to-peer), so Meta sees a URL token, not the content. The web page then does what the app does (WebCrypto key in the browser).
- WhatsApp Flows with an encrypted endpoint are a defensible middle ground for structured input (accept/counter/sign) because Meta signs the business's public key and the AES payload is decryptable only by the endpoint, but the final response forwarded to the webhook is not documented as hidden from Meta, so treat Flow outcomes as visible to Meta until verified.
- Never collect Aadhaar via chat; GigSync does not need it for job terms.

### Gaps

- Meta's WhatsApp Encryption Overview whitepaper and Hosting Terms not fetched; sub-processor list not checked.
- Whether India is a Local Storage region not confirmed.
- No official MeitY/Data Protection Board guidance on chatbots found; DPDP Rules commencement dates not verified here.

## Key Question 4: Signing and paying via WhatsApp

### Takeaway

A template can carry up to two URL buttons (2,000-char URL with one trailing variable), so a per-job signed link to a GigSync web page is straightforward; PDFs up to 100 MB can be sent as documents or template headers. UPI inside WhatsApp (WhatsApp Pay) has been uncapped for all Indian users since 31 Dec 2024, but there is no documented API for a business bot to request a UPI payment in-chat, so a UPI deep link/intent on the web page remains the practical route.

### Cited Findings

- Template buttons: up to 10 per template; up to 2 URL buttons, label 25 chars, URL 2,000 chars with at most one variable appended at the end (sample value required); up to 10 quick-reply buttons (25 chars); 1 phone button; 1 copy-code button; body 1,024 chars; footer 60; document header supported (uploaded via Resumable Upload API); no rule found that URLs must match the business domain — [Meta template components](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/business-management-api/message-templates/components) (read 28 Oct 2026).
- Templates with 4+ buttons or mixed quick-reply + other types cannot be viewed on WhatsApp desktop — same source.
- Document messages: PDF, DOC/DOCX, XLS/XLSX, PPT/PPTX, TXT; max 100 MB; optional caption 1,024 chars — [Meta document messages](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/cloud-api/messages/document-messages).
- WhatsApp Pay (UPI): NPCI removed the user-onboarding cap (previously 100M users) with immediate effect on 31 Dec 2024, subject to TPAP guidelines; the 30% UPI market-share cap was deferred to 31 Dec 2026 — [TechCrunch, 31 Dec 2024](https://techcrunch.com/2024/12/31/india-lifts-whatsapp-payment-curbs/); [MediaNama, Jan 2025](https://www.medianama.com/2025/01/223-npci-allows-whatsapp-pay-extend-upi-services-all-indian-users/). No 2026 reporting found on WhatsApp Pay's current share or on business-initiated UPI collect inside Cloud API for India.
- Flows can collect structured input (e.g. generate a quote from selections) and return it to the business via the encrypted endpoint — [Meta Flows](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/flows); [endpoint guide](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/flows/guides/implementingyourflowendpoint).

### Inferences

- Signing flow that keeps GigSync's promises: utility template "Job terms from <worker first name>" with one URL button carrying a one-time token; the web page (served by GigSync or opened from the worker's phone via a relay) renders the terms, generates/uses a WebCrypto key in the household's browser, signs the hash, and returns the signature to the worker's phone. Meta sees only the token URL. Browser-held keys are lost on cache clear, so the household's key should be treated as per-job, with the worker's signed record as the durable copy.
- Payment: a UPI intent link (`upi://pay?...`) or a PSP-generated UPI link on the same page; WhatsApp cannot be relied on to initiate a UPI collect from a bot. "Payments" messages exist in Cloud API for India (payments_in via Razorpay/PayU) in Meta docs as of 2024, but I did not verify the 2026 status: unverified.
- A PDF receipt of the signed terms can be pushed to the household as a document message (free inside the 24 h window / within the 1,000 free service messages; ₹0.115–0.145 as a utility template otherwise).

### Gaps

- Status of Cloud API "order_details"/UPI payment messages for India in 2026 not verified.
- Whether link previews or Meta's link-shimming alter tokenised URLs not checked.
- WhatsApp Pay P2M share and merchant-side API availability in 2026 not found.

## Key Question 5: Alternatives (SMS, RCS, Telegram, plain web link)

### Takeaway

SMS is cheapest-per-message-class after WhatsApp utility (~₹0.10–0.225 including the ₹0.025 DLT scrubbing fee) but needs DLT entity/header/template registration (₹5,900, 2–5 days entity, 7–15 days templates) and has unreliable delivery; RCS (Jio tiered from 1 Sep 2026) is ₹0.25 transactional and Android-only with patchy support; Telegram has ~87M Indian MAU (2022) skewed urban/high-income. WhatsApp's ~535M+ Indian users make it the only channel that reaches both low-income workers and households.

### Inferences

- For households, SMS with a link is the fallback that needs no app or opt-in beyond DLT consent records, at ~₹0.2/message; its weakness is deliverability and spam-folder filtering on Android, and the 7–15 day template-registration lag on wording changes.
- Telegram's free Bot API has no privacy advantage (bot traffic is not E2E) and its Indian user base does not match low-income household or worker demographics; useful only as a developer/testing channel.
- A dual-channel design (WhatsApp utility template first, SMS fallback on non-delivery) costs under ₹0.4 per notification and covers nearly every Indian mobile user.

## Key Question 6: Reliability and control risks

### Takeaway

Meta controls the channel end to end: quality ratings driven by user blocks/reports can cut messaging limits within 7 days, templates can be rejected, paused or re-categorised (marketing costs 7× utility), and WABAs can be disabled for "acceptable use" breaches with appeal windows of 24–48 h and no guaranteed reinstatement. For a worker-rights product this is a structural dependency: a dispute-heavy use case (households blocking a bot after a failed negotiation) feeds exactly the signals that degrade the number.

### Cited Findings

- Restrictions are graded: quality rating drops, messaging limit falls, templates get rejected or recategorised; the rating is based on blocks, reports, low engagement and delivery failures — [Chakra HQ](https://chakrahq.com/article/whatsapp-api-phone-blocked-business); [Messangi quality](https://support.messangi.com/hc/en-us/articles/39008595996187-How-WhatsApp-Business-Manages-Quality) (secondary).
- A red (low) rating moves the number to "Flagged"; if quality does not improve in 7 days, messaging limits are reduced; Flagged numbers cannot upgrade tiers — [Webex Interact](https://docs.webexinteract.com/docs/profile-statuses-1); [360dialog WABA health](https://docs.360dialog.com/docs/guides/maintain-waba-health) (secondary, consistent across vendors).
- Template review up to 24 h; templates with poor feedback are paused and, if low quality persists, disabled — [Messangi](https://support.messangi.com/hc/en-us/articles/39008595996187-How-WhatsApp-Business-Manages-Quality); read-rate is now part of the template quality rating — [MoEngage](https://www.moengage.com/blog/whatsapps-latest-deliverability-rules/).
- Triggers vendors cite: abrupt volume spikes, repeatedly rejected or sensitive-content templates, attempts to bypass limits, unofficial API use; Meta is said to disable suspected unofficial-API numbers proactively — [richautomate](https://richautomate.in/blog/whatsapp-unofficial-api-ban-risk-india-2026) (vendor claim, unverified).
- Disabled WABA: messaging stops immediately; appeal via Business Settings "request review"; vendors say Meta usually responds in 24–48 h; permanent bans generally not reinstated — [JustCall](https://help.justcall.io/en/articles/11122163-whatsapp-account-disabled-here-s-what-you-need-to-know); [Klaviyo](https://klaviyo.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/42141541588251-How-to-appeal-a-WhatsApp-Business-account-suspension); Meta developer community thread of a WABA disabled from Cloud API — [Meta community](https://developers.facebook.com/community/threads/806211104245421/).
- Without a payment method on file, service messages stop being delivered after the free tier; non-INR Indian accounts lose delivery from 1 Jan 2027 if not migrated — [Meta pricing](https://developers.facebook.com/documentation/business-messaging/whatsapp/pricing.md).
- A vendor blog claims 92M Indian WhatsApp accounts were banned in 2024; not verified against Meta's India monthly compliance reports — [richautomate](https://richautomate.in/blog/whatsapp-unofficial-api-ban-risk-india-2026) (unverified).

### Inferences

- GigSync's household side is adversarial by design (counter-offers, refusals). Households who block the number after a rejected negotiation directly lower the quality rating. Mitigation: send the household only one or two utility messages per job and move the negotiation to the web page; keep per-number volume low; split numbers by city.
- A Meta-rate-limited or disabled number is a single point of failure for every worker who chose WhatsApp. The worker-held signed record and the SMS/web fallback are what keep the product functional; the WhatsApp bot must be an optional transport, never the system of record.
- Nothing in Meta's policy prevents a worker-collective from running a WABA, but there is no precedent found of one doing so, and Meta's commerce/acceptable-use review is opaque; expect template rejections on wording that reads like a "service marketplace" without business verification.

### Gaps

- No documented case of an Indian gig/union bot being blocked was found; the risk is inferred from general policy, not from a precedent.
- Meta's exact messaging-limit tiers (250/1k/10k/100k) and current India tier-upgrade rules not fetched from Meta's page.
- Meta's acceptable-use decisions on "gig labour marketplace" category not found.
