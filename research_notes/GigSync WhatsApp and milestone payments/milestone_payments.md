# Milestone / stage-wise payment for multi-day gig and trade work in India (as of 28 Oct 2026)

Scope: how multi-day household trade work is actually paid in India, what the law says, what foreign platforms do, what UPI can carry, where workers get hurt, and which escrow-free protections have precedent. Written for a worker-first app that never holds money (household → worker directly via UPI; app records terms and countersigned receipts). Every claim is dated and linked; "[unverified]" marks claims from secondary/anecdotal sources or that I could not confirm against the primary text.

Tool-access note: labour.gov.in, indiacode.nic.in, support.upwork.com, npci.org.in product pages and help.fiverr.com returned 403/404 to my fetcher; those are cited via secondary mirrors and flagged.

## Q1. Market practice for household trades (advance, pricing basis, balance, retention, disputes)

### Takeaway

Organised Indian players converge on a two- or three-stage structure: a 5–35% advance (35% for Urban Company painting; 5%/₹25k or 10%/₹49k booking for interiors), one mid-stage payment at a visible checkpoint (framework/carcass done), and 50–65% on handover after inspection. Branded paint services and unorganised contractors leave terms to negotiation; a 30–50% materials advance is widely reported but I found no primary source quantifying it.

### Inferences

- The organised-market "shape" the app should mirror: small advance (≤35%, ideally materials-only), one checkpoint payment at a physically verifiable stage, and ≥50% on countersigned handover. A 35% advance already has consumer acceptance (UC), so a worker asking for it is not out of market.
- Retention in household work is informal (household simply withholds the last tranche); formal 5–10% retention is a B2B construction concept and would read as hostile to a 4-day painter. A better analogue: final tranche is released on inspection, not held for months.
- Single-visit trades (cleaning, beauty, small repairs) are universally pay-after-service; milestone logic only matters from ~2 days / ₹10k upward.

### Gaps

- No primary source quantifies the customary 30–50% unorganised-contractor advance; only anecdotes.
- Carpentry, plumbing, electrical and cook/maid monthly payment conventions (e.g. "paid on the 1st to 5th") were not found in any citable source; domestic monthly pay timing is a gap.
- Berger Express Painting, Housejoy and NoBroker renovation stage schedules not found.

## Q2. Law: minimum wages, wage period/timing, BOCW, Karnataka domestic workers bill, MSME 45 days

### Takeaway

All four Labour Codes came into force 21 Nov 2025; the Code on Wages sets wage periods ≤ 1 month and (per secondary summaries) daily-rated workers must be paid at shift end, weekly by the last working day, monthly by the 7th. Whether a household engaging a painter for 4 days is an "employer/establishment" under the Code is doubtful, so these are design norms, not enforceable obligations. Karnataka's 22 May 2026 minimum-wage notification gives ₹899–1,197/day in Bengaluru (Zone 1) depending on skill grade, but is under High Court challenge.

### Inferences

- A household hiring a painter for 4 days almost certainly isn't an "establishment" under the Code on Wages; the s.17 deadlines are therefore a credible default to encode (daily-rated → paid at end of each day; weekly → last working day) rather than a law to cite to households.
- A sole-proprietor painter with Udyam registration could, in theory, invoke MSMED s.15/18 against a household buyer; practically untested and unlikely for ₹5,000.
- Bengaluru skilled-rate floor ≈ ₹1,088/day (May 2026 notification) is a useful "is this quote below minimum wage?" check, with the caveat that the notification is sub judice.

### Gaps

- Could not read the bare-act text of ss.2, 16, 17, 45 (403); daily-wage deadline conflicts between "end of shift" and "before expiry of second day".
- Karnataka 2025-26 BOCW trade-wise rates (Painter/Mason/Carpenter) not captured.
- Outcome of the Karnataka HC challenge to the 22 May 2026 notification unknown.
- Draft Domestic Workers Bill: no wage-payment deadline text found; enactment status after Dec 2025 unknown.

## Q3. Platforms abroad: milestones, escrow, auto-release, disputes, retention

### Takeaway

Every major foreign platform solves the trust problem by holding money (escrow) and releasing per milestone with a 14-day default; the only ones that don't hold money (Thumbtack) push the deposit/half-way terms onto the pro's own contract. The transferable mechanics are: minimum-size milestones, submit-for-approval with a timer, partial-refund caps, and disputes that freeze only the contested tranche.

### Inferences

- Without escrow the app cannot replicate "auto-release"; the nearest equivalent is an auto-countersigned receipt: if the household does not object within N hours of a stage-complete submission, the stage is recorded as accepted and the UPI collect request for that stage is treated as due.
- Fiverr's "partial refund only on the first milestone" and Upwork's "min $5 milestone" both point to: keep milestone 1 small and make later milestones non-refundable once accepted.

## Q4. UPI mechanics for stage payments

### Takeaway

UPI already supports everything a non-custodial milestone schedule needs: a free P2P transfer up to ₹1 lakh per transaction, a transaction note / reference URL that can carry a stage ID, and the 12-digit RRN as a receipt key. AutoPay mandates to an individual VPA appear technically possible but I found no NPCI document confirming P2P mandates; recurring debits are now governed by RBI's E-mandate Framework 2026 (no AFA up to ₹15,000 per debit).

- A 4-day paint job of ₹40k fits inside P2P limits with room; only large renovations (> ₹1 lakh per stage) would need splitting across days or a merchant-category payee, which the worker won't have.
- The app should generate the UPI deep link / QR with `tn` = "GigSync <job-id> stage 2/3" and ask the household to paste the RRN (or screenshot) into the receipt; that gives a bank-traceable, countersignable record without custody.
- A daily/weekly AutoPay mandate from household to worker VPA is not something I can recommend as "supported" — it is unconfirmed for P2P, debits only in off-peak windows, and ₹15k no-AFA cap applies. Daily collect requests are safer.

### Gaps

- No NPCI circular found that explicitly permits or bars AutoPay mandates to individual (P2P) VPAs; frequencies (daily/weekly) for P2P mandates unverified.
- NPCI product pages returned no content; limits are cited via Razorpay/Pine Labs (June 2026), not the NPCI circular.

## Q5. Worker-side evidence of harm and what workers/unions ask for

### Takeaway

Documented harm concentrates on (a) opaque deductions and penalties on platforms, (b) non-payment by households/contractors with no cheap remedy, and (c) advance-fraud by contractors that then poisons households' willingness to pay advances to honest workers. Hard incidence data for household-trade non-payment is thin; the best numeric evidence is India Labourline recoveries and the Economic Survey's earnings data. I found no union demand specifically for daily or weekly settlement in household work.

- Fairwork India 2025 not found; current India Labourline totals not readable.

## Q6. Escrow-free protections with precedent and the realistic ₹5,000 remedy

### Takeaway

There is precedent for every escrow-free lever: small first milestone (Upwork min-milestone, Livspace 5–10% booking), pay-at-checkpoint tied to a visible stage (HomeLane framework/carcass, Livspace "wood framework completion"), inspection before final payment (UC "final inspection with you"), third-party attestation (consumer-commission treating platform as principal; Labourline mediation), and photo/receipt evidence (UPI RRN). For a ₹5,000 household claim the only routes that actually move are a free helpline/union mediation or a police cheating complaint; the Code on Wages s.45 authority is cheap on paper but its applicability to a household employer is unsettled.

### Inferences (design-ready)

- Default schedule for a 4-day paint job that matches market and law: Day 0 materials advance ≤ 35% against a materials receipt; end of each day a countersigned day-receipt (no money if lump-sum, money if daily-rated); Day 2 checkpoint (prep/primer done, photo) 30%; handover after walk-through 35%. Nothing retained after handover; snag list recorded instead.
- Acceptance timer: stage submitted with photo → household has 24 h (not 14 days; the work is physically in their home) to object or countersign; silence = accepted in the record, and the UPI collect request for the stage is marked overdue.
- The receipt should store: stage ID, amount agreed, amount paid, UPI RRN, timestamp, both signatures. That record is exactly what Labourline conciliators and a s.45 authority need, and it is what a consumer commission looked at in the May 2026 order.
- A union/circle attestation (worker's circle vouches that the worker's stage photos are consistent) is the escrow-free substitute for Airtasker's dispute desk; precedent is thin, flag as experimental.

### Gaps

- No evidence on what actually works for a ₹5,000 household non-payment: no helpline breakdown by claim size, no small-cause-court data.
- No published Indian precedent for "silence = acceptance" of a stage in household work; the 24-h figure is my proposal, not sourced.
- Whether a household is an "employer" under Code on Wages s.2 for a multi-day tradesperson is unsettled; needs a lawyer, not more search.
