# India gig-work platforms: what they actually give workers (state as of 27 Oct 2026)

Scope note: India only. "Announced" = company/press claim; "operating" = corroborated by a second source, a worker account, or a regulator. Web search quota was exhausted before Yes Madam, BookMyBai, Apna/WorkIndia, Ola (current) and Uber India (current) could be verified from primary pages; those rows are flagged. No Fairwork India 2025 report exists in any index checked (fair.work, CITAPP/IIIT-B); the 2024 edition (Oct 2024) is the latest anchor.

## Key question 1: What concrete benefit does each platform provide, who is eligible, what are the limits, and when was it announced? (announced vs operating)

### Takeaway

Only five platforms (bigbasket, Swiggy, Urban Company, Zepto, Zomato/Blinkit) offer company-paid accident + health cover with loss-of-pay; ride-hailing (Uber, Ola, Rapido, Porter) offers little beyond legacy or marketing-copy insurance and has moved to subscription/zero-commission models instead. Rest shelters are real at Amazon (Ashray) and Zomato (5,000+ "rest points"); earnings guarantees exist only as a minimum-wage-after-costs policy at bigbasket and Urban Company (and a contested ₹20,000/132-hour InstaHelp promise); worker stock exists only at Urban Company (PSOP, 2022); housing/relocation help exists at no platform.

### Inferences

- The benefit stack is bifurcated by sector: food/grocery delivery and home services carry a real, company-paid insurance layer (because of Fairwork pressure, 2020 pandemic precedents and now statute), while ride-hailing and logistics have shifted cost to drivers via subscriptions and push risk back onto the driver's own vehicle insurance.
- Most "benefits" are tied to being active (insurance "for all active partners"), which makes them lapse exactly when a worker is injured, relocating or between platforms; only the e-Shram UAN is portable.
- Marketing figures (Blinkit "₹10 lakh medical", Zepto "₹10 lakh", Rapido "₹5 lakh") exceed the amounts disclosed in audited/press-released policies (Zomato medical ₹1 lakh; Swiggy hospitalisation ₹2 lakh); the headline number is usually accidental death, not health.

## Key question 2: What do workers and worker groups say about these benefits (criticism, uptake, gaps)?

### Takeaway

Unions and worker surveys consistently say platform benefits are conditional, hard to claim, and undercut by the things platforms do not provide: pay floors, protection from ID blocking, and recognition; the January 2026 and February 2026 strikes were about those, not about insurance amounts.

### Cited Findings

- Fairwork 2024 worker interviews: rising fuel costs without matching fee changes; AI-based complaint systems discourage grievances; slot-booking since 2022 reduces flexibility and carries demotion/penalties; "workers in different categories are prioritised differently for grievance redressal, loss of pay, and insurance schemes" — [Fairwork 2024 PDF](https://citapp.iiitb.ac.in/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/Fairwork-India-2024-Report.pdf)
- "Though platforms claim to provide health insurance for gig workers, these benefits are often subject to performance and earnings"; one study found only 1 in 5 gig workers have life or health cover — [Outlook Business](https://www.outlookbusiness.com/corporate/the-unprotected-millions-bridging-the-health-insurance-gap-for-indias-gig-workforce)
- TGPWU 2024 impact report (published Aug 2025): systemic issues are wage theft, arbitrary deactivation/ID blocking, absence of social security, poor safety; 62% of surveyed workers got support from the union itself (scholarships, ration kits, accident-insurance help, e-Shram and ITR help) rather than from platforms — [BHRRC](https://www.business-humanrights.org/en/latest-news/india-unions-report-highlights-gig-and-platform-workers-struggles-with-wage-theft-unfair-deactivations-lack-of-social-security-unsafe-working-conditions/)

### Inferences

- The gap workers name most is procedural (deactivation without appeal, opaque payouts), which no insurance product fixes; this is consistent with Fairwork giving zero Representation points to every platform.
- Union capacity is partially substituting for platform welfare (TGPWU delivering scholarships and insurance help), which suggests demand for a worker-side, platform-independent support layer.

### Gaps

- No quantitative insurance-claim uptake data from any platform except Swiggy's maternity-claim split (75% spouses).

## Key question 4: What changed in 2025–2026 (state gig laws, Social Security Code, 10-minute delivery pressure, strikes)?

### Takeaway

2025–26 is the shift from voluntary platform welfare to statutory levies: the central Code took effect 21 Nov 2025, Karnataka started collecting a 1% welfare fee from 13 Feb 2026 (now in litigation, with platforms ordered to deposit fees in court), Telangana enacted its own Act, e-Shram onboarding became mandatory (deadline 21 June 2026), and the Centre pushed quick-commerce firms to drop "10-minute" branding after the NYE strike, with limited practical effect.
