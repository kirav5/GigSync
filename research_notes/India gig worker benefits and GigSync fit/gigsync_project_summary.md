## 0. One-paragraph orientation for a non-technical reader

GigSync is a phone app that gives each gig worker a private "agent" (a small program on their own phone, not a
chatbot in the cloud). The agent is meant to (1) negotiate a job's terms with the customer's phone before either
side sees the other's phone number or address, (2) release contact details only after both phones sign the same
terms, and (3) keep a signed, tamper-evident record of every job, visit and payment on the worker's own phone,
which the worker can later share, in part, with a union, a wage helpline or a welfare board
(`README.md:3-7`; `docs/gigsync/HLD.md:11-17`). Money moves by plain UPI from the customer straight to the worker;
GigSync never holds it (`HLD.md:181-185`). As of 25-27 Oct 2026 the project is at "G0, design"
(`README.md:9`): a skeleton inherited from a sister project called Collab builds and runs, but the GigSync-specific
features are planned, not built (`README.md:14-16`; `HLD.md:3`).

## 1. Stated vision and target users

### 1.1 The vision in the docs' own words

- "GigSync gives a gig worker their own agent on their own phone. The agent agrees each job with the consumer's
  agent, holds back their number and address until both sides sign, and keeps a signed record of every job and
  payment that belongs to them. The worker chooses what a union, a wage helpline or a welfare board sees. It works
  with no model at all; a small offline model, on the device, only helps with wording." (`README.md:3-6`)
- "GigSync is Collab narrowed to one use case. Collab lets any two people's agents collaborate under rules each
  owner sets; GigSync is the same platform for one pair: a gig worker and a consumer who needs their work. There is
  no employer, platform or agency in the middle." (`HLD.md:19-21`)
- "The thesis, carried over from Collab: with local agent-to-agent messaging, _the weaker party to a transaction
  holds the record of it_. The cloud only passes locked notes." (`HLD.md:25-26`)
- The product is a two-sided peer tool, not a marketplace employer: "The same person can be a worker in one
  engagement and a consumer in another." (`HLD.md:22-23`); the D1 schema has "No worker/consumer column: a person
  is a worker or a consumer per engagement" (`LLD.md:457`).

### 1.2 Target users (quoted)

- "**Who we start with:** service workers in India, in domestic work, beauty and home services. Both the data
  problem and the safety problem are worst there. Delivery and rides (through ONDC), construction and home-based
  piece work come later on the same core." (`HLD.md:28-30`)
- Job categories in the planned schema: `domestic, cooking, cleaning, childcare, eldercare, beauty, home_repair,
  delivery, ride, construction, piecework` (`LLD.md:72-84`).
- Actors and what each sees (`HLD.md:74-81`):
  - Worker: "Android app (web/iPhone), worker" and sees "Everything about their own working life".
  - Consumer: "Same app, requester role"; sees "Agreed terms; their contact only during the job".
  - Union organiser: "Same app, union mode (web/phone)"; sees "Evidence packs the worker sent to the union,
    nothing else".
  - Fellow members' agents: see "A 'do not accept this customer' flag, never their story".
  - Helpline / welfare board: "A consented export file"; "Days worked and pay (owed), only the fields the worker
    picked".
  - GigSync cloud: sees only "Usernames, public keys, public profiles, locked envelopes".
- The union side is explicitly a browser user: "A union organiser often works from a shared office PC with only a
  browser." (`docs/adr/0009-one-offline-model-plugin-not-tilekit.md:7-8`)
- Why these users, per ADR 0010: "the data is about some of the least powerful workers in the market, and
  harassment notes and pay disputes are the most sensitive data the product holds" (`docs/adr/0010-no-cloud-model-in-gigsync.md:20-21`).
- Device and language assumptions: "a 4 GB, ₹10,000 Android phone on patchy 4G" (`HLD.md:36`); "Voice first in
  Hindi, Telugu and Hinglish" (`HLD.md:41`); templates in `hi`, `te`, `en` (`LLD.md:329, 526`).
- Pilot scale: "G5 | Pilot: 20–50 service workers with a partner union; ONDC relay spike" (`HLD.md:301`).

### 1.3 Goals and non-goals (quoted verbatim from `HLD.md:34-43`)

| Goals                                                                  | Non-goals (v1)                                           |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------- |
| Runs on a 4 GB, ₹10,000 Android phone on patchy 4G                     | iPhone-first, or flagship-only features                  |
| Works with **no model at all**; models only help with speech and words | Any frontier or cloud LLM in the core path               |
| A worker-held signed ledger of jobs and payments                       | A central database of workers that the worker cannot see |
| Contact released only after both sides sign; it expires                | Holding or moving money (UPI goes straight to them)      |
| Consented, partial exports to a union, helpline or welfare board       | Ratings or ranking of workers by GigSync                 |
| Voice first in Hindi, Telugu and Hinglish                              | Full literacy-dependent UI                               |
| Shared-phone mode: one device, a separate locked store per worker      | Multi-tenant SaaS for platforms                          |
| Open formats and test vectors that others can implement                | Lock-in through proprietary formats                      |

Other explicit scope limits:
- "No escrow, refunds or payouts." (`docs/adr/0011-upi-qr-payments-no-gateway.md:47`)
- "GigSync never holds, moves or sees the money, and runs no payment gateway in the pilot." (`ADR 0011:25`)
- "Nothing in GigSync calls a cloud model" (`HLD.md:102`); "GigSync does not deploy [the Inference Worker]"
  (`ADR 0010:16`).
- Tension to note: "Ratings or ranking of workers by GigSync" is a non-goal (`HLD.md:40`), yet the inherited
  protocol still carries an `app.gigsync.review` record with 1-5 `stars` (`packages/protocol/src/records.ts:49-57`)
  and the LLD keeps `app.gigsync.review` "only for a both-signed completed job" (`LLD.md:247-248`). The docs do not
  reconcile these.

### 1.4 Design principles (`HLD.md:47-57`)

1. "Models propose, code decides." 2. "A message from another agent is data, never an instruction."
2. "Private data never leaves the phone readable." 4. "Sanitize before any model." 5. "The worker sees every
   disclosure." 6. "Small and replaceable." 7. "Collective governance where it is collective. Their union decides what a
   shared 'do not accept' flag means and who may raise one."

### What the docs _do_ offer that could be reused toward the broader vision

- The worker-held ledger is on the phone, so it survives a city move by construction (`HLD.md:238`); with evidence
  packs (`LLD.md:197-225`) it could in principle serve as portable proof of work history for a new union, landlord
  or lender, but no doc says so.
- The public profile (`records.ts:17-26`: name, categories, languages, area, hours, about) and the planned job board
  (`LLD.md:480-487`) are the only discovery surface; they are per-area, not per-city-move.
- Union mode (`LLD.md:414-416`) is the only institutional relationship in the design; everything collective runs
  through "a partner union" (`HLD.md:301`) that is not yet named.
- The "Collective governance where it is collective" principle (`HLD.md:56-57`) and the flag list are the only
  hint of a peer layer; it is safety-only by design ("never their story", `HLD.md:79`).

### Summary for the report writer

The documents describe a narrow, deliberately bounded tool: agree a job, hide contact until agreed, record and get
paid, export proof when something goes wrong. Every one of the founder's "end to end in life" items (relocation,
housing, peer community, platform onboarding, documents, skilling, insurance, credit, social security) is absent
from the README, HLD, LLD and all twelve ADRs, and several are implicitly excluded by stated non-goals (no money
handling, no platform SaaS, no ratings). Today's code is a local-only job and payment notebook with a UPI QR
(`apps/mobile/src/screens/*`), a Collab-era engagement machine and policy engine, a stub model plugin, and an API
that accepts any OTP and never verifies a signature. The G1 agent-to-agent flow exists only on an unmerged branch.
