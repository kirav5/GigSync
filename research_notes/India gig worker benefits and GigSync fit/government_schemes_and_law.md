# Government and legal benefits, registrations and protections for gig and platform workers in India (as of 27 October 2026)

Reading guide: every row states the status as **passed / notified / operational** where it could be determined. "Operational" means money or a benefit is actually reaching workers, or a portal is live and accepting registrations. Where a status could not be confirmed from a primary or dated secondary source it is flagged as unconfirmed and listed under Gaps. Figures are dated as of the source's reporting date, not today.

## Key question 1: Code on Social Security 2020 — gig/platform worker definitions, the aggregator cess, and what is live today

### Takeaway

The Code on Social Security 2020 (with the other three Labour Codes) came into force on 21 November 2025 and the implementing Social Security (Central) Rules 2026 were notified on 8 May 2026, so gig/platform workers are now a legally defined category with a mandatory aggregator-data-sharing regime on a central portal. However, as of the latest sources (mid-2026) the aggregator contribution rate and its start date under Section 114(5) have **not** been notified and **no** benefit scheme under Section 114(1) has been notified, so no money is flowing and workers have no central-Code benefit to claim yet.

**Definitions (Code on Social Security 2020)**
- Gig worker: a person who earns from work arrangements outside the traditional employer–employee relationship; platform worker: a person engaged in work through an online platform; aggregator: "digital intermediaries connecting service providers with consumers" — [DD News](https://ddnews.gov.in/en/what-indias-new-labour-codes-mean-for-gig-and-platform-workers/)
- Gig and platform workers remain outside the EPF, ESI and gratuity chapters that apply to "employees"; the Code creates a separate scheme-based architecture for them — [Chambers (KSK)](https://chambers.com/articles/epf-compliance-in-the-gig-economy-is-indias-new-social-security-framework-enough-2); [Labour Law Reporter FAQ](https://labourlawreporter.com/gigworkers.asp)

### Inferences

- "What is live today" for a gig worker under the central Code is: (a) legal recognition, (b) a mandatory data-sharing/registration pipeline from aggregators into e-Shram/Shram Suvidha, (c) an eligibility rule. There is no central cash or insurance benefit flowing from the aggregator fund because neither the rate, the start date nor any Section 114 scheme has been notified. Any product telling a worker they are "covered" under the Code would be premature.
- The 90/120-day rule means a worker's engagement days per aggregator per financial year is the key eligibility record; a worker-side log of daily earnings per platform would directly serve future claims.

### Gaps

- No Gazette of India notification for the Section 114(5) start date or the turnover-rate could be found through October 2026; status is inferred from the most rigorous secondary source (KSMG) and MediaNama (May 2026). Should be checked on labour.gov.in/egazette before publication.
- Official PIB pages on this topic (PRID 2200767, 2220307, 2303053) returned HTTP 403 to automated fetch; figures are taken from outlets quoting them.
- The exact gazette number for the Social Security (Central) Rules 2026 was not captured.

## Key question 2: e-Shram portal — registrations, what registration gives, Budget 2025 gig-worker ID cards and PM-JAY, 2026 status

### Takeaway

e-Shram has 31.89 crore registrations (18 Aug 2026) but only about 10.5–12 lakh (roughly 1.05–1.2 million) are platform/gig workers, i.e. well under 10% of the Economic Survey's 12-million gig workforce. Registration gives a UAN/e-Shram card, PMSBY accident cover (Rs 2 lakh) and a single window to 15 schemes; the Budget 2025 promise of gig ID cards plus PM-JAY was still described as "not yet launched for platform workers" in May 2026, with a January 2026 Rajya Sabha reply describing the e-Shram–PMJAY integration as in place technically.

### Inferences

- The gap between ~1.05–1.2 million registered platform workers and the ~12 million gig workforce (Economic Survey) means roughly 9 in 10 gig workers are not yet in the registry that the Code, PM-JAY and most state boards will key off. Registration itself is the single most valuable action a product can prompt.
- The jump from 5.12 lakh (1 Dec 2025) to 10.5 lakh (23 Jan 2026) coincides with the Labour Codes' commencement and the aggregator API pipeline; the subsequent plateau (~12 lakh by Jun–Aug 2026) suggests aggregator bulk uploads stalled or were partial, consistent with the Ministry's complaint that aggregators had not uploaded entire workforces.
- A dedicated gig ID card distinct from the ordinary e-Shram UAN card was not found; the "ID card" promise appears to be fulfilled, if at all, by the e-Shram card itself.

### Gaps

- No official source confirming PM-JAY cards actually issued to gig workers specifically, or a count, as of October 2026. Latest clear statement (May 2026) says not launched.
- PIB releases PRID 2220307 and 2303053 and labour.gov.in PDFs could not be fetched (HTTP 403); numbers rely on outlets quoting them.
- Whether the Ministry still pays the first-year PMSBY premium for e-Shram registrants in 2026 is unverified.
- Official e-Shram "gig worker" count as a precise number (not "around") was not found.

## Key question 3: State laws — Rajasthan, Karnataka, Telangana, Jharkhand, Bihar, Tamil Nadu, Delhi, Maharashtra

### Takeaway

Six states have enacted or passed gig-worker statutes (Rajasthan 2023; Karnataka 2025; Bihar 2025; Jharkhand Act published 6 Jan 2026; Telangana passed 30 Mar 2026; Maharashtra bill introduced 3 Jul 2026), but only Karnataka has the full stack visibly operating: rules (Nov 2025 and Mar 2026), a constituted Board (Jan 2026), a notified welfare fee (effective 13 Feb 2026) and a live grievance portal (IPGRS, May 2026). Rajasthan's board and fund exist on paper and a 2026 journal says a welfare tax was imposed, but no notified fee rate, rules date or collection figures could be found; Tamil Nadu has a board without a statute and below-1% enrolment; Delhi has only a budget line and an announcement.

### Inferences

- For a worker moving between cities, Karnataka (Bengaluru) is the only destination where a state-level unique ID, fee-funded board and online grievance route demonstrably operate as of October 2026. Rajasthan, Bihar and Jharkhand have laws but no evidence of money or IDs reaching workers; Telangana, Maharashtra and Delhi have nothing operational.
- Every state law keys registration to the platform sharing a worker database, and every state reports aggregator data-sharing as the bottleneck (Tamil Nadu explicitly). A worker-held record of platform engagement would let the worker register and prove eligibility without waiting for the aggregator.
- The fee bases diverge (Karnataka and Telangana: % of each payout; Jharkhand and the central Code: % of turnover), which will complicate any cross-state portability of entitlements; the central Code does not pre-empt state funds and the EPW piece treats them as parallel.

### Gaps

- Rajasthan: no 2025–2026 primary source on rules notification, Board meetings, cess rate or collections. The Vision IAS/Indian Express "how other states fared" page (1 Apr 2026) returned HTTP 503 and could not be read.
- Karnataka: exact assembly passage and assent dates for the Act (vs. ordinance) not confirmed; number of workers/aggregators registered with the Board not found; a claimed "Rs 4 lakh insurance" figure could not be sourced.
- Jharkhand and Bihar: no evidence of Board constitution, rules or fee start-date notification.
- Telangana: no evidence of gazette notification/assent after 30 Mar 2026.
- Delhi: no evidence the board was constituted after the May 2025 announcement.
- Maharashtra: full bill text (fee %, termination, algorithm provisions) not read.

## Key question 4: Migrant-worker-facing schemes relevant to a gig worker moving city

### Takeaway

ONORC is the one migrant scheme that functions at scale (201+ crore portability transactions by Feb 2026), though inter-state portability is a tiny fraction of the total; ARHC rental housing has effectively stalled (about 5,600 of 83,534 flats converted). State migrant registries exist in Kerala (Athidhi, 3.72 lakh), Tamil Nadu (12 lakh+) and Jharkhand (SRMI/Shramadhan), but they are destination-state labour registries not linked to gig status; PM Vishwakarma does not cover gig workers and PMKVY has no gig-specific track found.

### Inferences

- For a gig worker moving inter-state, ONORC is the only central entitlement that transfers automatically; it needs an NFSA ration card and a seeded Aadhaar, and inter-state use is rare enough that fair-price-shop staff may be unfamiliar with it.
- Destination-state registries (Athidhi, TN portal) are employer- or contractor-driven; a self-employed platform worker has no "employer" to register them, so these registries largely miss gig migrants unless the worker self-registers.
- Housing support via ARHC is effectively unavailable in Delhi and Mumbai, the two largest gig labour markets.

### Gaps

- No 2025–2026 ARHC figures found; latest dashboard figure is from a 2023 article.
- "Shramik Kalyan" could not be matched to a specific central scheme; nothing found.
- No Odisha or UP migrant-specific portal found; may exist under other names.
- No evidence that any state migrant registry is linked to e-Shram's gig-worker flag.

## Key question 5: Numbers — NITI Aayog estimates, newer estimates, share by sector, share who are migrants

### Takeaway

NITI Aayog (June 2022) estimated 7.7 million gig workers in 2020-21 rising to 23.5 million by 2029-30; the Economic Survey 2025-26 (Jan 2026) put the figure at 12 million in FY25 (over 2% of the workforce), with 40% earning under Rs 15,000/month. The only sourced migrant share is IDinsight's 2024 survey of 2,547 two-wheeler delivery workers: 51% migrants (24% intra-state, 26% inter-state). Fairwork's latest India ratings found are 2024.

## Key question 6: Grievance and helplines — India Labourline, state labour helplines, evidence a worker needs

### Takeaway

India Labourline (1800-833-9020, run by Working Peoples' Charter with Aajeevika Bureau since 2021) is the only national worker-facing helpline with published outcomes (3,600+ cases and about Rs 2.1 crore recovered by June 2022; Rs 2.41 crore in 18 months), and it works by phone mediation then field visits then legal notice. Karnataka's IPGRS is the only live statutory gig-grievance portal (May 2026). No official checklist of evidence for gig-worker complaints was found anywhere; the only documented intake requirement (Karnataka) is name, address, mobile number and a brief description, with the platform's committee free to ask for documents.

### Inferences

- Because no formal evidence checklist exists, the practical evidence set is dictated by what mediators need to call an employer/platform: the worker's identity and registration (e-Shram UAN / state UIN), the platform and partner/rider ID, dated earnings statements or app screenshots of payouts and deductions, the deactivation or penalty notice, and chat/email with support. Karnataka's dashboard of "payments, welfare fees and eligible schemes" is the model for what a worker-side record should capture.
- Labourline's method (phone mediation first) means a worker who can produce the platform's own payout record on the call is in a far stronger position than one relying on memory; Labourline's own case data suggest most disputes are wage/payout disputes, not deactivations.

- No current (2025–2026) outcome statistics for India Labourline found; latest are mid-2022.
- State labour helpline numbers for Karnataka, Telangana and Maharashtra not found in search; need direct check of state labour department sites.
- Whether 155214 operates nationally or only in Delhi is unresolved.
