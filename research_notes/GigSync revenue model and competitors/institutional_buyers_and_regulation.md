# Institutional buyers and regulatory constraints for a worker-first gig app in India (as of 27 Oct 2026)

Scope: who, other than the worker and the household, could realistically pay for GigSync (worker-held agent, signed job/payment record, e-Shram/board registration help, migrant landing via vouched partners, union-run circles, no per-job cut), and what regulation prices or blocks each line. All claims dated and linked; "UNVERIFIED" flags secondary or estimate-only sources. Research method: ~60 web searches/fetches, Oct 2026. Primary-source PDFs (Karnataka Act 72 of 2025, Khaitan note on RBI PA Directions, TGPWU 2024 impact report) could not be text-extracted in this environment, so their contents are cited through PRS, law-firm and press summaries.

---

## KQ1. Government and welfare boards: budgets, fee collections, what they buy

### Takeaway

State gig-worker welfare funds are real but young: Karnataka's 1% levy (live from 13 Feb 2026) is officially projected at ₹250–300 crore/year, but no actual collection figure has been published; Rajasthan has never notified a rate; Telangana's Act passed in 2026 with no notified rate; the central CoSS aggregator contribution (1–2% of turnover) has no commencement date. The one proven government-pays-per-registration precedent is e-Shram's ₹20 per worker to CSC VLEs (~₹347 crore paid to Dec 2022). No evidence was found of any board procuring a worker-facing app or paying NGOs per grievance.

### Inferences

- The only government money with a proven per-unit price for the thing GigSync does (helping a worker register) is e-Shram's ₹20/registration via CSC — and that channel is closed to non-CSC entities. A per-registration deal with a state board would need a new procurement; Karnataka has instead built its verification system with an academic/philanthropic partner (IIIT-B, Azim Premji Foundation), not a vendor.
- Karnataka's fund (₹250–300 crore/yr projected) is legally spendable only on "schemes" for registered platform gig workers, with admin capped at 5%; household-hired domestic workers are outside its Schedule, so GigSync's core users may not be beneficiaries at all unless the Domestic Workers Bill passes.
- The Karnataka Rules (machine-readable worker database, 7-day join/exit reporting, worker dashboards) create a compliance tooling need for _aggregators_, not a budget for worker apps.

### Gaps

- No actual welfare-fee collection figure for Karnataka (Feb–Oct 2026), nor any Board annual report.
- Rajasthan: no notified rate, no fund balance, no board spending found.
- Telangana final Act: rate/base not confirmed from the text (PDF not parsed).
- No example found of any Indian state board contracting an NGO/startup per registration or per grievance; CSC's ₹20 is the only per-unit precedent.
- Haqdarshak government-contract pricing not public.
- Bhashini grant sizes not found; NITI Aayog has no funded gig pilot found.

## KQ2. Unions and worker organisations: what they can pay; who funds them

### Takeaway

Indian gig/domestic unions run on tiny dues (SEWA historically ₹5/year; no published fee found for IFAT, TGPWU, AIGWU, GigWA) and project grants; none has a visible tooling budget. International funders that have paid for worker-data/worker-tech in India exist (Mozilla Data Futures Lab up to $100k awards with gig work an eligible domain; Omidyar India ₹7.5 crore COVID fund; Ford ~$37M/yr global Future of Work(ers)), but no 2025–26 India gig-worker tech grant programme was found. The realistic framing is union-as-channel funded by a philanthropy, not union-as-customer.

## KQ3. Insurers and lenders: distribution commissions and paying for consented income data

### Takeaway

Insurers can legally pay an app for distribution only if it is a licensed intermediary (POSP under a licensed entity, corporate agent, broker or web aggregator); since April 2023 IRDAI has no product-wise commission caps, only an overall expenses-of-management ceiling (30% of gross premium for general insurers, 35% health-only), so commission is negotiable board-to-board. Lenders pay for consented data through the Account Aggregator rails (market rates ~₹5–15 per consent/fetch, UNVERIFIED; standardised fee from 1 Nov 2026), and RBIH's gig-worker lending pilot shows appetite for platform activity data — but none of this is a revenue line for a non-licensed app.

### Cited Findings

**Insurance**
- IRDAI (Expenses of Management, including Commission, of Insurers) Regulations, 2024 effective 1 Apr 2024; Master Circular IRDAI/F&I/CIR/79/5/2024 of 15 May 2024; consolidates 2023 EoM and Commission regs — [Taxguru](https://taxguru.in/?p=902360); [Outlook Money](https://www.outlookmoney.com/amp/story/personal-finance/irdai-provides-guidance-on-remuneration-to-insurance-distributors).
- March 2023 reform: product-wise commission caps removed; insurers pay under board-approved policy subject to overall EoM cap of 30% of gross written premium (general) and 35% (standalone health); rewards/incentives count as "commission"; review every three years (next 2026) — [Legal500/Mondaq](https://www.legal500.com/intelligence/india/insurance/new-regulations-on-payment-of-commission-and-expenses-of-management); [Business Standard, Mar 2023](https://business-standard.com/finance/insurance/irdai-s-act-of-commission-new-rules-give-insurers-greater-flexibility-123033100764_1.html). - A worker-held signed job/payment ledger is exactly the "alternative data" RBIH/Vivifi underwrite on, but the money flows FIU→AA/FIP, and a phone app is neither. ## KQ5. Regulatory constraints on each candidate revenue line

### Takeaway

A non-intermediary app that only records jobs and never touches money sits outside the three heaviest regimes (RBI payment-aggregator authorisation with ₹15/25 crore net worth; TDS under s.393(1)/old 194-O; state welfare fees), because each hinges on "facilitating"/"connecting" buyer and provider or handling funds — but GigSync's "agent that agrees jobs with households" does connect buyer and provider, which is the literal test for "aggregator" under both CoSS s.2(2) and the Karnataka Act. GST at 18% applies to any platform/subscription/commission revenue above ₹20 lakh; DPDP's purpose-limitation and consent rules (fully effective 13 May 2027, penalties up to ₹250 crore) constrain data sales but not consented data portability.

### Inferences

- "Would a non-intermediary app that only records jobs be an aggregator?" Taxmann's reading of CoSS s.2(2) is that the test is whether the platform _connects_ buyer and provider; pure record-keeping does not obviously meet it, but "merely hosting" is not obviously outside it either — the answer turns on function. GigSync's agent "agrees jobs with households" is a connecting/negotiating function; the defensible position is that the _worker's own agent_ (running on the worker's phone, no central marketplace, no matching) is not a "digital intermediary or marketplace". No case law or ruling found either way.
- Welfare-fee exposure in Karnataka is small even if caught: 1% of payout capped at ₹1.50 per transaction for "professional activity providers".
- The PA and TDS regimes are avoided entirely by never taking custody of money — household pays worker's UPI directly; the app only records the signed receipt. The explicit RBI carve-out language was not located; treat as the consensus industry reading, UNVERIFIED.
- DPDP does not forbid a worker _exporting_ their own record to a lender (data principal's own act); it forbids the app repurposing it. "Data-as-income-proof" revenue is therefore structurally a worker-initiated port, not a sale.

### Gaps

- RBI PA Directions 2025 exact exemption text (P2P, delivery-vs-payment, QR display) not retrieved; needs the Master Direction PDF.
- Karnataka Act Schedule text not read directly (PDF not parsed); PRS says no domestic/household entry, but confirm.
- Whether Consumer Protection E-Commerce Rules 2021 amendments were ever finalised — not confirmed.
- No GST advance ruling on "worker-side subscription with no commission" found.

## KQ6. Willingness-to-pay evidence: workers, households, benchmarks

### Takeaway

There is no Indian survey measuring what gig/domestic workers or households will pay for safety, verification or contracts; the best proxies are Namma Yatri's driver subscription (₹25/day unlimited or ₹3.50/trip; 35,000 of 89,573 drivers enrolled at launch, 7,500 unprompted, Sept 2023), Urban Company Plus (price reported anywhere from ₹299/yr to ₹999–1,999/yr, "1 million subscribers" — all UNVERIFIED), and Care.com household memberships ($11–39/month). Workers trade wages for job security in one urban India study, and verification failure (20% fail address checks) is a documented household-side pain.

**Worker-side paid plans**
