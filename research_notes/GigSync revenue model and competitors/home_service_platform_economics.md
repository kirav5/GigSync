# Business and revenue models of India's home-services and instant-help platforms (as of 27 Oct 2026)

Scope: Urban Company (core marketplace + InstaHelp), Snabbit, Pronto, Broomees, BookMyBai, Yes Madam, Housejoy/Dunzo (failures), Apna/WorkIndia (contrast), Swiggy/Zomato rider economics (contrast). All currency INR unless stated. "Unverified" marks figures from a single secondary source or a company claim not checked against filings. Where I compute a number from cited inputs it is labelled "derived" and the arithmetic is shown.

Reading note for the writer: Urban Company is the only company in this set with audited, public, quarterly disclosure (listed Sept 2025). Snabbit's only hard numbers are RoC filings for FY25/FY26. Pronto has disclosed no revenue at all. Everything else on Snabbit/Pronto pricing and pay is press-reported, time-stamped, and has moved repeatedly in 18 months; treat any single number as a snapshot.

---

## Key question 1: What is each platform's take rate, and what does a worker net per hour after platform deductions, fines and costs?

### Takeaway

Urban Company's core marketplace takes roughly 28% of what professionals gross (commission + fees) and ~36% of NTV on a blended revenue basis (incl. product sales); its active professionals net ~₹311/hour on the company's own definition. The three "instant help" products (InstaHelp, Snabbit, Pronto) currently have _negative_ effective take rates — customer pricing (₹1–₹200/hour, mostly ₹99–₹169) sits at or below what workers are paid (~₹100–₹135/hour logged, ₹21,000–₹40,000/month for 8–12 hour days), with the gap funded by investors; the only stated steady-state target (UC's) is ₹200/hour price, ₹150/hour payout, ₹50/hour costs, i.e. a 25% take that equals cost.

- Snabbit and Pronto disclose no take rate, no gross billing, and no per-hour pay schedule; Pronto discloses no revenue at all.

## Key question 2: Which revenue lines are extracted from the worker versus the customer?

### Takeaway

Urban Company's core model is unusual in how many lines it draws from the worker side (commission, time-boxed subscription plans, pre-paid lead credits held as a float, mandatory branded products and kits, paid re-training, loans) on top of customer commission, convenience fee and memberships; the instant-help models (InstaHelp, Snabbit, Pronto) extract almost nothing from workers except uniforms and leave/lateness deductions, and instead under-price the customer; the agency models (Broomees, BookMyBai) and job boards (Apna, WorkIndia) charge only the customer/employer.

### Cited Findings

- **UC worker-side lines (2021→2025):** Dec 2021 subscription system: ₹2,000–5,000 token fee, tiers Flexi/Smart/Plus, customer discount up to 10% "funded from partners' fees" — [Inc42, 22 Dec 2021](https://inc42.com/buzz/urban-company-sues-partners-protesting-against-its-subscription-system-calls-protests-illegal-violent-prone/). Jul 2025: ~₹1,000 per 10-day plan, Prime/Lux, credits ₹10 each with required balance ₹2,500–3,500, mandatory branded products scanned per job, kits ₹40–50k, company loans for kits, unpaid month-long training, part of earnings "converted into the required credit balance" — [Newslaundry, 7 Jul 2025](https://www.newslaundry.com/2025/07/07/long-hours-low-earnings-paying-to-work-the-brutal-life-of-an-urban-company-beautician). UC's own blog (COVID-era) said it was "removing the automatic credit deduction and making it optional for all" — [UC blog, 12-point program](https://www.urbancompany.com/blog/urban-company-announces-12-point-program-to-improve-partner-earnings-and-livelihood).
- **UC product sales as revenue:** product sales were 20.77% of FY22 revenue (₹91 Cr) and 22.13% of FY23 (₹141 Cr) — [Al Jazeera, 1 Mar 2024](https://www.aljazeera.com/economy/2024/3/1/how-indias-urban-company-has-soured-gig-work-for-women); ₹188 Cr FY25 (residual) — [Entrackr, 18 Jun 2025](https://entrackr.com/fintrackr/urban-company-posts-rs-1144-cr-revenue-and-rs-285-cr-pbt-in-fy25-9373371); ₹66.2 Cr product revenue vs ₹51 Cr B2B2C product cost in Q1 FY27, i.e. ~23% gross margin on products sold to professionals — [Inc42, 4 Sep 2026](https://inc42.com/features/inside-urban-companys-marketplace-model-what-it-keeps-from-every-booking/). ### Inferences

- UC's credit float (₹2,500–3,500 mandatory balance × tens of thousands of active beauty partners) is effectively interest-free working capital from workers; combined with product gross margin (~23% in Q1 FY27) and training/kit sales, the worker-side lines are material even though UC does not report them as such.
- The instant-help trio have shifted extraction from the worker to the investor: zero subscription/lead fees, but wages set by platform fiat and tuned downward (Pronto Nov 2025; Snabbit's "upward of ₹40,000" became "₹25,000–30,000" within five months).
- Pronto Verified is the first case in this set of a platform monetising the _work itself_ (video) to a third party rather than the customer or worker; if it reaches anything like the reported 90% of revenue, Pronto's unit economics stop being a home-services question.

### Gaps

- No rupee-level disclosure of UC subscription/credit/training revenue exists in public filings; the ₹1,000/10-day and ₹2,500–3,500 credit figures are from worker interviews (Jul 2025) and may vary by city/category.
- Whether Yes Madam's per-booking platform fee is charged to the customer or the partner is not stated.
- Pronto Verified contract values and named buyers were not found (Forbes article returned 403).

## Key question 5: Which regulatory costs now hit these models?

### Takeaway

Four layers now apply: Karnataka's 1% welfare fee on every worker payout (live since Feb 2026, capped at ₹1.5/transaction for professional-services platforms like UC), the central Code on Social Security aggregator levy of 1–2% of turnover capped at 5% of worker payouts (Code in force 21 Nov 2025; rate/notification still being operationalised), TDS under s.194-O at 0.1% of gross facilitated amounts (since 1 Oct 2024; 5% without PAN), and GST at 18% on platform commission/fees plus s.9(5) reverse liability making the e-commerce operator the deemed supplier for housekeeping (plumbing, carpentry) services by unregistered workers.

### Cited Findings

- **Karnataka:** Karnataka Platform-Based Gig Workers (Social Security and Welfare) Act, 2025, s.20 allows 1–5% welfare fee on every payout; state notified 1% by Government Order gazetted 13 Feb 2026 (Deccan Herald: payable from 13 Feb; Storyboard18: effective Monday 16 Feb 2026); per-transaction caps: ₹0.5 food/grocery delivery (Swiggy, Zomato, Blinkit, Zepto, BigBasket), ₹0.5/₹0.75/₹1 logistics by vehicle, ₹1.5 for professional service providers "including Urban Company"; aggregators self-declare and pay quarterly; tips and incentives excluded (Rule 17) — [Deccan Herald, Feb 2026](https://www.deccanherald.com/india/karnataka/swiggy-zomato-to-pay-welfare-fee-to-gig-workers-as-karnataka-notifies-1-levy-on-aggregators-3900681); [Storyboard18, Feb 2026](https://www.storyboard18.com/brand-marketing/karnataka-imposes-1-welfare-levy-on-swiggy-ola-and-other-aggregators-89903.htm); [SCC Online, 18 Feb 2026](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2026/02/18/karnataka-government-notifies-gig-workers-welfare-fee-mandatory/). Court challenges by platforms reported but status unverified — [IndianHRM guide](https://www.indianhrm.com/guides/gig-workers-social-security-code).
- **Code on Social Security, 2020:** in force 21 Nov 2025; aggregators contribute 1–2% of annual turnover to a social security fund, capped at 5% of amounts payable to gig/platform workers; rate per aggregator category by notification; secondary sources say Central Rules notified 8 May 2026 with 45-day registration/upload of workers — [Fisher Phillips](https://www.fisherphillips.com/en/insights/insights/indias-new-labor-codes-extend-social-security-coverage-to-gig-workers); [DD News](https://ddnews.gov.in/en/what-indias-new-labour-codes-mean-for-gig-and-platform-workers/); [KSMG explainer](https://ksmg.co/explainers/labour-codes/gig-worker-social-security-new-labour-codes); [SNS & Co blog (rules date, unverified)](https://snsco.in/blog/gig-platform-worker-rights-social-security-code-guide.html).

### Inferences

- The Karnataka cap structure (₹1.5 on a ~₹1,500 UC core order ≈ 0.1%; ₹1.5 on a ₹114 InstaHelp order ≈ 1.3%) means the fee is trivial for high-ticket marketplaces and bites hardest on micro-ticket instant help — the opposite of its headline "1%".
- The central 5%-of-payout cap on the aggregator levy is the number that matters for shift-wage models (Pronto, Snabbit) where almost all cost is payout; for UC core, 1–2% of turnover is lower than 5% of payouts.
- s.9(5) makes a platform GST-liable on the full value of housekeeping services by unregistered workers (i.e. nearly all), so an 18% GST on gross service value is already embedded in InstaHelp/Snabbit customer prices (if they are treated as ECOs) — a structural ~15% headwind versus an offline maid; Pronto's employer-like model would instead be a direct supplier charging GST on its own turnover.

### Gaps

- No category-wise notification of the central aggregator contribution rate (1% vs 2%) was found as of Oct 2026.
- Litigation status of the Karnataka Act challenge not verified.
- No source states how UC, Snabbit or Pronto actually classify themselves for GST (ECO under s.9(5) vs principal supplier), nor the effective GST on an InstaHelp hour.
- Whether 194-O or GST TCS is netted before or after platform fees is unresolved in the sources.
